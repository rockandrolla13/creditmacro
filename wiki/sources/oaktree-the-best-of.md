# The Best of . . .

## What this source is

A retrospective anthology: Howard Marks' own pick of his strongest Oaktree memos across
35 years (1990–2025), released October 2025 for his memo-writing anniversary. Each listed
memo gets a one-paragraph editorial gloss. The collection splits into two kinds — statements
of enduring investing principles, and after-the-fact chronicles of major episodes (the
dot-com boom/bust, the 2007–08 credit crisis, the 2022 rate-regime shift). It is reasoning
and reflection, **not** a single dated, investable market call.

## Why it matters

It is a compact map of the mental models Marks returns to, and therefore a **method** source
for how a disciplined investor frames cycles, risk, and edge. Several of its principles run
parallel to this engine's own discipline (asymmetry, outcome-range risk, anti-forecasting),
so it is useful for calibrating the method layer. It also functions as an index: a pointer to
~40 underlying memos that could each be ingested on their own.

## Core theme candidates mentioned

These are **method principles**, not investable theme candidates:
- Second-level thinking — edge comes from thinking differently *and* more correctly than the crowd.
- Cycles and the pendulum — sentiment swings (greed↔fear) are among the market's most reliable
  features; the task is to gauge where in the cycle you stand, not to predict the turn.
- Risk as the *range of possible (including adverse) outcomes*, explicitly not volatility, and
  not reducible to a single number.
- Asymmetry as the objective — returns more than commensurate with the risk borne; framed as
  the cornerstone of superior investing.
- The limits of macro forecasting — shared macro knowledge confers no edge; prefer "I don't know."
- Price versus intrinsic value — a bargain needs an irrationally motivated seller; paying a fair
  price does not by itself create alpha.
- Luck versus skill — outcomes are noisy; judge process over single results.
- Risk control rather than risk avoidance, especially in credit ("fewer losers" over "more winners").

## Extracted causal claims

Mechanism-level, paraphrased:
- Abundant capital + eagerness to deploy → weaker underwriting ("race to the bottom") → seeds
  of later losses.
- Risky/volatile assets + excessive leverage + weak holder staying power → fragility → forced
  selling under stress.
- Psychology overshoots (from "hopeless" to "flawless") → price detaches from value → contrarian
  opportunity is greatest at the extremes.
- High starting valuations → lower subsequent returns (value exerts a long-run pull on price).
