## April 2 

Prospect of oil production cuts boosts equities and crude, overshadows 6.65mm weekly unemployment claims; Treasuries weather coupon increases, IG issuance to close modestly higher; USD, petrocurrencies strengthen; Services and composite PMIs, US Employment report ahead; US 10y at 0.597% (+1.4bp) 

## _Our US Public Policy team has authored a CARES Act Primer_ 

S&P (2527) climbed 2.28% on Thursday, led by a 9% advance in energy stocks as the prospect of an oil truce and output reductions lifted WTI crude to settle 24.7% higher (at $25.32/bbl), overshadowing another unprecedented surge in US weekly jobless claims (6.65mm). A relatively narrow 4-8bp intraday range left Treasury yields 1-3bp higher (5y 0.381%, +3bp) with increased coupon sizes and a heavy slate of IG issuance pricing (~$32bn to bring the weekly total to a record $110.9bn in just four days) weighing on the curve. Supply in France and Spain weighed on the EGB complex as record tenders for UKT 2041s saw Gilts outperform on the margin. USD appreciation was only outmatched by petrocurrency strength as NOK outperformed G10 peers. 

An extension of Wednesday’s risk-off backdrop failed to materialize even after the US president warned of dire circumstances in the weeks ahead and the administration contemplated domestic flight restrictions (if only to ‘hot spots’). NIAID director Fauci’s assessment that the country appeared to be on track with regards to vaccine development (eyeing a 12-18m timeline) and confirmation that a White House meeting would take place with oil industry executives on Friday helped to soften the tone. The IRS clarified that Social Security recipients would not be required to file in order to receive individual aid disbursements and HUD announced hardship relief for single-family homeowners with FHA-insured mortgages. With most of the move coming before the close, Treasuries extended Wednesday’s late rally on the Fed’s SLR announcement (see our initial synopsis here and Thursday’s daily update here) only slightly, to mark a 0.565% 10y yield low. 

Governments in Asia continued efforts to thwart the spread of COVID-19 and mitigate its economic impact. Thailand announced daily curfews ahead of a cabinet meeting on Friday, while South Korea launched its bond support fund as the BoK might consider lending to non-bank financial firms if needed. Taiwan’s cabinet intended to add NT$250bn to its NT$100bn special budget, which would bring its relief package to NT$1.05tn, including NT$700bn in loans. In China, where data from the Finance Ministry indicated that 83.5% of this year’s local government bond quota had already been issued by the end of 1Q, headlines that the state would buy oil to shore up reserves gave both equities and crude a lift heading into London trading, where Brent would rise nearly 13%. In Europe, EC President von der Leyen authored an apology to Italy in La Repubblica for the EU’s inadequate initial response to the coronavirus outbreak there. The UK’s FCA proposed a temporary (3m) holiday for certain credit card borrowers; the FT would later report on a new emergency lending scheme for midsized companies. After breaching 2500, E-minis dipped not long after the head of Germany’s KfW opined on the severity of the crisis and expected applications (currently standing at €10.6bn) to accelerate in the near term. OATs led EGB yields higher as €15bn of issuance from France and Spain (including the lowest 30y bid-to-cover in Bonos since December 2008) stood in contrast to a record bid-to-cover in UK’s 20y offering. With Spain reporting a record daily mortality figures (sending Treasury and longer-dated Bund and Gilt yields lower) and the ECB extending its strategy review through mid-2021, French Finance Minister Le Maire advocated €200bn in additional EIB financing to help address the region’s economic crisis. As New York trading was set to begin, German Economy Minister Altmaier expected a 2020 GDP contraction in excess of 5%. 

Treasury benchmarks stood virtually unchanged heading into the initial jobless claims release. The latter surpassed even the highest estimates compiled by Bloomberg, doubling last week’s record at 6.648mm to bring the two-week combined total just shy of 10mm (9.955mm, or 6% of the BLS total civilian labor force). The release showed 18 states reporting more than 100k unemployment filings spread across industries. While E-minis would drop to as low as 2425 ahead of the exchange open, the rates reaction was virtually nonexistent. An earlier drop in 3mL (1.373%, -6.35bp) alleviated pressure on front-end spreads while no take-up of the Fed’s 11-day term funding (and minimal participation in both overnight operations) pointed to a calm funding backdrop; bill yields would steadily climb through $220bn 4w, 8w, and CMB (102d and 154d) supply. 

The president’s announced expectation that Saudi Arabia and Russia would agree to curb output after speaking with their respective heads of state (and SPA headlines of Saudi Arabia’s request for an OPEC+ meeting) vaulted equities into the green and pushed WTI crude gains as high as 35% on the day. Russia’s contention that it had not been in contact with Saudi Arabia regarding output cuts saw equities, oil, and yields retrace a portion of their advance; credit indices, which had gapped tighter, also pared gains. Meanwhile, an increase in coupon sizes for next week’s 3y (+$2bn), 10y (+$1bn), and 30y auctions (+$1bn) came against another crowded new issue slate as the curve bear-steepened. Remarkably, Thursday’s $19bn jumbo offering would not even be the largest of the week, and EUR-denominated issuance amount to half of last April’s in just the first two days of the month. 

At midday, the announcement that the US DoE would make SPR storage available (separately, US transit authorities would also be granted emergency aid) and stabilizing case data in Italy would bring S&P back within 10pt of its morning peak by 1pm. Nevertheless, a postponement of the Democratic convention until August, potential FEMA equipment shortages, and possible

Politico reported that most of the ventilators ordered by the White House would be ready in June, and Russia would purportedly base revised 2020 budget plans assuming $20/bbl oil (inviting revenue shortfalls and increased OFZ issuance). 

Risk assets would fare better late as a Texas Regulator spoke with Russia’s Energy Minister and discussed 10mm barrel/day cuts, Lipper reported record weekly inflows into HY funds ($7.09bn), and the president directed HHS to facilitate procurement for ventilator production. After the morning’s jobless claims data, Cleveland Fed Pres. Mester believed U3 could even eclipse 15%. Late session data releases from the Fed showed ~$177bn of FX swap lines tapped between March 26 and April 2 while Treasuries held in its custody fell a record $109bn in March; the Fed’s balance sheet also grew to $5.81tn. By 5pm, modest daily rate moves masked a 6.5-16.5bp bull steepening in TIPS that widened breakevens 8.5-19bp on the day. Services and Composite PMI readings and the US March Employment Report will highlight Friday’s calendar. 

## **Developed Markets** 

Another quiet session in **Japan** saw both cash and swaps bull flatten against a continuation of the risk-off move overnight and a stronger than expected **JGB 10y** auction (A: 100.75; C: 100.71). Real money investors appeared to drive the long-end outperformance. Primary dealers and JGB investors meetings were held at the **MoF** to discuss the additional JGB issuance that will finance Japan’s supplemental budget to address the coronavirus outbreak. Media reports suggested that market participants may be able to absorb additional issuance of as much as ¥16.9tn (corresponding to the totals announced in spring 2009 to fund GFC economic stimulus measures). **USD/JPY** struggled to find direction in Tokyo after being weighed down by risk-off sentiment overnight, trading in a 107.00-107.60 range. Though short-end USD/JPY basis suggested further easing of USD funding conditions, there was little urgency to chase USD/JPY higher. 

In **Australia** , ACGB yields rose across the curve at 8:15pm ET as the **RBA** reduced the pace of ACGB purchases as part of its 3y yield targeting framework. Previous RBA purchases of ACGBs in long-dated open market operations (data here) had been conducted at a pace of at least A$3bn per day. RBA purchases of A$2bn were the smallest since the program started on March 20. July 2022 yields rose 1bp to intraday highs of 0.2300% and May 2030 yields rose 6bp, to 0.7554%. The RBA has reduced ACGB purchases incrementally from A$4-5bn initially to A$3bn, and Thursday’s purchase will likely influence market expectations toward anticipating A$3bn or smaller daily ACGB purchases. The move higher in ACGB yields had little influence on AUD, which also did not react to data showing a decline in both job vacancies and business confidence 15 minutes following the RBA purchase announcement.

**EUR/USD** broke below the March 24 high of 1.0880, suggesting the pair may have more downside momentum. The pair has moved from the top end of the channel, at around 1.1150, to the middle (the lower end would be 1.0700). **GBP/USD** was unchanged on the day, failing to break above the 1.25 area, which is a psychological resistance level. The pair is not overbought around this point (based on 14-day RSI), but there are no support levels to note. A break below 1.2245 would likely be seen as a bearish sign by markets. 

A heavy **EGB** supply day saw **France** and **Spain** together issue over €15bn of debt across maturities, both at the very top of their pre-announced target ranges. Semi-core underperformed across the curve, with **OATs** seeing a substantial concession into the long-end auctions (10y almost 8bp cheaper intraday, closing at 0.04%, +4.3bp). The levels of investor interest were comparable to the ones seen earlier this year (2.67x bid/cover vs 2.17 previously on the 2029s). Some of the losses were reversed in the afternoon, with 10s30s bear-flattening  4.4bp to 69.6bp. 

The underperformance of **Bonos** against the rest of periphery was led by the front end, with 5y (0.507%, +1.2bp) cheapening the most on the day. **BTPs** were mostly range-bound, with 30y particularly well-bid, rallying 6.9bp on the day, to 2.394%, as 5s30s flattened ~7bp, to 151.5. In core countries, the belly underperformed, with 2s10s in Bunds bear-steepening to 20.6bp (+3.1bp) and 10s30s down 3.2bp, at 39.8bp. **Gilts** traded sideways, cheapening after the open, rallying into the 20y supply (which was well received by the market), and losing some gains in the afternoon in line with global duration.  10y closed 1.8bp cheaper at 0.33%, with curves marginally steeper. 

In the **dollar bloc** , FX moves were relatively limited until **AUD** , **NZD** , and **CAD** reached intraday highs, following headlines indicating China would start buying oil for its state reserves. Oil reached intraday highs as well, following the US president’s statement that he “expects and hopes” that Saudi Arabia and Russia will cut oil production. WTI oil prices consolidated around $25/bbl, up from around $22/bbl in NY morning trading but below post-statement highs around $27/bbl as a spokesman for the Kremlin denied that Russia and Saudi Arabia were in communication regarding the oil market. CAD gains were limited relative to other oil exporting nations’ currencies as **CAGBs** (2y +2bp, 10y + 5bp) broadly tracked Treasuries and Western Canada Select prices remained below $10/bbl. Canada issued C$4bn June 2030s at 0.661% and **USD/CAD** closed roughly unchanged.

- The **ECB** issued a statement officially postponing all the events associated with its **strategy review** (including the listening events of the ECB and the NCBs) to the second half of 2020. - In an interview with CNBC, **Dallas Fed President Kaplan** commented that, while any potential agreement on production would ease pressure on oil in the short run, “we’re going to be spending a substantial amount of time working off the high level of oversupply”. Kaplan expected the unemployment rate to climb into the low- to mid-teens before settling back below 10% (and closer to 8%), though further labor improvements would take time. Asked what additional steps the Fed is considering, Kaplan indicated that the central bank was working “at breakneck speed” to set up its Main Street Business Lending Program. ## G4 Smarter (beta) Trading Strategy 

Enhancements to a G4 10y government bond futures momentum strategy have produced higher Sharpe ratios and stronger returns, relative to total return government bond indices for the G4, US, Germany, Japan, and the UK since 2000. See A "Smarter" (Beta) Way to Trade G4 10y Futures Duration? for more information on these strategies. 

## **Trading Strategy 1 – "Trade Longs/Fade Shorts"** 

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract and hold for a 25-business-day period. In short, this strategy buys futures when the Simple Moving Average Crossover (SMAX) generates both a long and a short signal, given the historical outperformance of long signals traded long and underperformance of short signals traded short. Given that the SMAX could generate both a long and a short signal within the predefined holding period, an investor may have a 200% long position since each of the two signals would be traded in separate portfolio sleeves. 

## **Trading Strategy 2 – Trade "Longs Only"** 

When the 5-day moving average crosses above the 20-day moving average, buy the futures contract (long duration) and hold for a 25-business-day period. When the 5-day moving average crosses below the 20-day moving average, do nothing. In short, an investor ONLY trades long signals initiated by the SMAX given their historical precedent to outperform.
