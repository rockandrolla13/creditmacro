## **What We’re Watching** 

## **What’s Notable This Week:** 

• Our economists expect euro area 4Q GDP releases to show continued softness in the data, partly driven by transitory factors such as the protests in France and the weakness in the German car industry. Headline inflation likely fell again, while core inflation is expected to stay unchanged at ~1%Y. In the UK, Tuesday’s parliamentary vote on the way forward for Brexit will be in focus. Our economists now expect the Commons to vote to extend the Article 50 period if parliament does not vote for a deal by mid-February. 

• Last week’s credit performance was strong but characterised by a decompression trend. While EUR IG spreads tightened by 3bp, HY spreads widened by 3bp. This translated into excess returns of +32bp and -1bp, respectively. Within IG, short-duration and better ratings cohorts outperformed, while corporate hybrids lagged. In HY, better-quality credit outperformed, with the B-BB basis widening by ~5bp. 

• EU IG mutual fund flows were broadly unchanged last week, while HY funds experienced modest net inflows. Meanwhile, issuance gathered momentum, driven by select jumbo deals. Non-financial corporates issued €10bn of EUR IG and €2bn of EUR HY bonds.
