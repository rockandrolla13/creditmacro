## **IG Fundamentals: High Grade, Mid-Cycle** 

**European credit fundamentals remain on track:** Across the 165 high grade issuers that we track, credit quality continued to show broad improvement through 4Q10. Deleveraging was especially strong, with the median net debt/EBITDA of the market now at the lowest level of the last decade. 

**A variety of credit cycle metrics say mid-cycle:** A composite view of balance sheet trends, M&A activity and bank lending surveys is hovering near early 2004 levels. These trends, along with growing risks from central bank rate hikes and margin pressure, suggest a ‘mid-cycle’ credit environment, one that will continue to reward relative value strategies. 

**Update of market valuations:** We update our ‘spread-per-leverage’ valuation measures for the overall high grade market. The large reduction in gearing has caused the market to now look slightly cheap relative to 10year averages, although we note substantial differences at the sector level. 

**Screens and investment ideas:** Moving from sectors to single-names, we provide screens of best and worst credits on the basis of fundamental trend and adjusted valuation. While we continue to think that the current cycle will drive more dispersion, we also think that the risks of a stronger EUR, higher oil and rising rates will be relatively tougher on high yield, at least on a tactical basis. 

Please see _**High Grade, Mid-Cycle**_ , published on March 25, 2011.

## **IG Fundamental Improvement Continues Apace** 

- While 4Q10 earnings often disappointed equity analysts’ high hopes, their trend remained positive. EBITDA growth and the rapid accumulation of cash balances have flattered credit metrics. 

- Median IG net leverage fell nearly a third of a turn QoQ, and nearly a full turn since the peak in leverage five quarters prior. About 80% of issuers reported YoY deleveraging as well as improving interest coverage ratios. 

- Liquidity remains strong as corporates continue to hoard cash, reflecting their conservatism in the face of macro uncertainty.

## **IG Valuation Picture** 

- The combination of strong deleveraging and range-bound spreads means that high grade credit is looking more attractive on our spread-per-leverage (SPL) valuation metric. Dividing non-financial spreads by median issuer leverage, European IG cash credit offers ~90bp of z-spread per turn of gearing, compared to a historical average 71bp (see bottom left). 

- ‘SPL’ is a slow-moving valuation reading, given that the denominator is based on LTM data. For a faster-moving valuation band, we look at spreads relative to European volatility (VDAX), which should to a greater degree capture the more immediate risks circling markets. Scattered together, they can provide a long-term and short-term composite of valuation. Adjusted for balance sheet and market risk, high grade credit markets still look modestly cheap relative to the last ten years.

## **Other Ways to Play: Switches Into Hybrids, and Tactical IG/HY Decompression** 

- We continue to view corporate hybrids as an attractive way of leveraging a positive high grade fundamental story (see _Global Credit Strategy: Corp Hybrids: The Road Less Travelled_ , February 25, 2011). Granted, we were also of this view before the market’s recent weakness, but think that the recent back-up in hybrids presents a buying opportunity, especially in light of recent S&P rule changes which look to make the issuance of current ‘high-step’ structures less likely going forward. Below we show two switches between hybrids of reasonable fundamental stories and convexity profiles, versus conventional long-bonds. 

- Finally, we would prefer to position for the risks of rising rates, higher energy prices and a stronger EUR though a view that European high yield _relatively_ underperforms investment grade on a tactical (three-month) view, rather than shorting either market outright. Given the generally smaller size, higher domestic concentration and weaker pricing power of high yield issuers, we see the market as relatively more exposed to higher oil and a stronger EUR. The experience of the US credit market, where US high yield has struggled relatively more following the start of a central bank hiking cycle, also informs this view (see _Taking a Hike_ , March 11, 2011).
