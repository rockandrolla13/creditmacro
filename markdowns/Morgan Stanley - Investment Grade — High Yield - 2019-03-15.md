**In this week’s note, we provide a brief update of IG, HY, and loan fundamentals** 

## **Investment Grade** 

- **Gross leverage ticks lower:** Median IG leverage declined modestly in 4Q18 to 2.28x  as EBITDA grew at 11.4%, while debt grew 5.3%. IG leverage is still considerably higher than past “pre-recession” levels. 

- **It gets more difficult from here:** An earnings recession likely began in 1Q19. Unless debt stops growing, IG leverage should rise in 2019 and off of a higher starting point than past periods of rising leverage. 

- **Cash/debt still falling:** Cash/debt is now at the lowest level since 4Q08 as buybacks, dividends, M&A, and capex all rose in 2018. Lower cash levels drove IG net leverage up to 1.77x last quarter. 

- **Interest coverage ticks up:** Interest coverage increased to 10.74x in 4Q18, as strong EBITDA growth offset higher debt levels and higher coupons. However, IG interest coverage is still well below the 2014 peak. 

## **High Yield** 

- **HY leverage declines in 4Q18:** HY gross leverage dropped by 0.15x in 4Q18 to 4.07x for the median HY issuer, on the back of 11.1% y/y LTM EBITDA growth and relatively subdued total debt growth. HY leverage is still at the high end of the range from 2004-2015, but it is now 0.66x below the 2016 peak. 

- **HY interest coverage improves, cash/debt drops:** Interest coverage ticked higher to 4.67x in 4Q18, back near peak levels last hit in 2015. Cash/debt declined, and now sits below the long-term median. 

## **Leveraged Loans** 

- **Elevated loan leverage:** For the existing universe, loan leverage declined in 4Q18, though by less than the decline in HY leverage. However, new issue 1L leverage increased to 4.18x in 2018 (from 3.92x in 2017). 

- **Loan interest coverage also higher:** Loan interest coverage increased to 4.19x in 4Q18 (vs. 4.12x in 3Q18). Loan cash/debt is now at 7.34%, the lowest level since 2013.

## **Investment Grade Gross Leverage Declines, but Net Leverage Rises** 

- **Median IG leverage declined modestly in 4Q18 to 2.28x – down both since last quarter and y/y.** Strong EBITDA growth for US companies outpaced debt growth. However, total debt still increased for most companies, meaning leverage fell mostly because of earnings. 

- **Net leverage rose to 1.77x as IG cash levels fell broadly.** Cash declined for the majority (60%) of IG companies in our database, with the trend not specific to just the largest issuers who benefitted most from repatriation. 

- **Most sectors saw a decline in leverage** , with only Utilities, Communication Services, and Healthcare increasing leverage y/y. Technology, Energy, Materials, Consumer, and Industrials all had lower gross leverage. 

- **The challenges will grow going forward.** As we discuss later on, earnings growth may turn negative for several quarters in 2019. Hence, unless total debt stops growing, which is unlikely, IG leverage may again start rising in 2019, and off of a higher starting point than past periods of rising leverage.

## **Investment Grade EBITDA Growth Was Solid, but Debt Still Growing** 

- **Gross leverage declined as EBITDA growth has outpaced debt growth for the last three quarters.** LTM EBITDA for our constant universe grew 11.4% y/y, while total debt grew 5.3%. 

   - EBITDA growth in our constant universe reached its highest level since 3Q11. 

   - Debt growth has trended lower since 3Q15, but ticked higher last quarter. 

- **However, most companies are not shrinking their debt outstanding, at least through 4Q18.** By count, 54% of companies in our full universe saw debt levels increase vs 4Q17 by a total of $422bn, vs 46% of companies who had lower debt, but only by $214bn in aggregate. 

   - Excluding the beneficiaries of repatriation, of which the largest have stopped issuing debt, 57% of the remaining companies had higher debt levels. 

- **Additionally, 60% of companies in our full universe saw cash balances fall** by $420bn in aggregate (biased by changes at some of the largest IG names) contributing to the increase in net leverage. 

   - Although 37 of the repatriation names accounted for $248bn of the decrease in cash, there were over 185 other issuers with declining cash balances.

## **Investment Grade Implications of an Earnings Recession** 

- **Our equity strategists believe an earnings recession has started.** As discussed in US Equity Strategy: Weekly Warm Up: Earnings Recession Is Here (2019.02.11), consensus forecasts for the S&P 500 point to declining earnings in the first quarter (y/y) and also in the second quarter when excluding financials. 

   - Although consensus EPS shows a considerable bounce in the second half, especially 4Q, our equity strategists have remained more bearish, expecting no more than 1% earnings growth in FY19: US Equity Strategy: Weekly Warm Up: The Evidence Is Building for Further Slowing. Correction Likely Not Over (2019.03.11) 

- **If earnings play out according to consensus forecasts, and companies don’t grow debt, IG leverage could come down slightly in 2019. But we think this outcome is unrealistic.** Companies will likely continue to grow debt at a modest pace, and if the recovery in earnings in 4Q is more moderate, IG leverage will start rising again.

## **Investment Grade Levera e Trends Still Broad-Based g** 

- **The trends we observe for our IG universe can be seen on both broader and more granular levels.** For example, US corporate debt-to-GDP, calculated using Fed data, is also near alltime highs. 

- **As we have maintained, the rise in leverage has been broadbased across ratings – not just a “high-quality” problem.** Although leverage broadly ticked down this quarter, BBB-rated companies actually had modestly higher leverage. Declines in leverage were concentrated in the higher ratings cohorts. 

- **While the 4.0x+ leverage bucket peaked in 2Q16, about 25% of total debt still falls in this range,** up modestly from last quarter. The second-most levered bucket (3.00-3.99x) has also grown in recent quarters. Growth in the tail has been modestly offset by growth in the lowest leverage cohort.

- **Interest coverage ticked up to 10.74x in 4Q18, as strong EBITDA growth counteracted the impact of higher debt levels and higher coupons** . Interest coverage has broadly been on the decline since 2014, as debt levels grew amid tepid earnings (in 2015-2016 in particular) and, more recently, rising financing costs. 

   - About 65% of the companies in our database had higher LTM interest expense as of 4Q18 vs 4Q17. 

• **We note that the tailwind from being able to refinance into lower coupons is likely behind us, given higher Treasury yields and wider IG spreads.** As we noted in Corporate Credit Research: Update: Uncovering Rising Interest (2018.04.13), higher yields will take time to noticeably impact interest coverage, but we are starting to see at least a modest impact already. For example, the average coupon on the IG index is now trending higher after falling all cycle. 

- **Bigger picture, high interest coverage should no longer be used as a way to rationalize high leverage – it is now noticeably below pre-crisis peaks.** And as we have pointed out in the past, the Fed cut rates by over 500bp in the last two cycles, and the 10Y dropped by over 3% (peak-to-trough). When the cycle turns this time, declining yields may not provide the same tailwind to interest coverage on the way down.

## **High Yield HY Leverage Declined in 4Q18** 

- **HY gross leverage declined by 0.15x in 4Q18 to 4.07x for the median HY issuer** . Leverage is still at the high end of the range from 2004-2015, but it is now 0.66x below the 2016 peak. HY net leverage declined by a similar amount, though is still 0.2x above the long-term median. 

- **HY leverage ex-commodities declined by a more modest 0.07x** , after sitting in a tight range for the past two years. Note, HY ex-commodity leverage, at 4.14x, is now above leverage for the full market for the first time since mid-2015, given the steady decline in leverage for the Energy sector over the past two years. 

- **At the sector level** , median gross leverage increased q/q for Consumer Goods, Gaming/Leisure, Retail, Metals & Mining, and Autos/Parts; it decreased q/q for Energy, Industrials, Media, Tech and Paper/Packaging; it was roughly flat for Transportation, Chemicals, and Healthcare. 

- **It gets harder from here.** The decline in leverage in 2018 came on the back of very strong earnings growth. However, 1Q19 likely marks the beginning of an earnings recession, according to our equity strategist. Hence, unless total debt growth turns negative, expect leverage to again start rising in 2019, and off of a higher starting point than past periods of rising leverage.

## **High Yield HY Total Debt Growth Remains Lackluster** 

- **Total debt growth slowed again in 4Q both for the broader market and ex-commodities.** HY debt growth slowed to 1.8% y/y in 4Q18 (from 3.7% in 3Q18). - **The median Y/Y change in EBITDA was positive for all but two sectors.** EBITDA grew fastest for Energy, Tech, Telecom, and Industrials and grew slowest for Consumer Goods, Transportation, Media, and Gaming/Leisure. As mentioned on the previous slide, earnings growth has been strong on a backward-looking basis. But going forward, we expect much weaker earnings trends. Earnings revision breadth has been some of the worst our equity strategists have ever witnessed, with both sales and margin guidance coming down across all sectors.

## **High Yield HY Interest Coverage Ticks Higher, but Coupons and Interest Expense Also Higher** 

- **High yield interest coverage improved modestly.** Median interest coverage ticked higher to 4.67x in 4Q18 (from 4.63x in 3Q18), back near peak levels last reached in 2015.

Average HY yields were also up over the same period, but by a slightly lower amount. That said, a change in yields takes time to filter through into interest coverage for the HY market in aggregate, as we outlined in: Corporate Credit Research: Update: Uncovering Rising Interest (13 Apr 2018). The coupon on the HY index increased slightly on the quarter to 6.41% (from 6.40% in 3Q18), the second quarterly increase after 31 straight quarterly declines. Similarly, interest expense as a percentage of total debt ticked higher last quarter as well. 

- **However, despite higher yields, total debt only grew 1.8% y/y in 4Q18** , placing just marginal upward pressure on the growth in total interest expense. More importantly, EBITDA grew at 11.1%. EBITDA has been rising faster than debt for the past eight quarters, helping to drive the increase in interest coverage.

## **Leveraged Loans Loan Interest Coverage Rises; Cash/Debt Declines** 

- **Interest coverage increased to 4.19x in 4Q18** (vs. 4.12x in 3Q18), below the cycle peak of 4.37x in 1Q15. 

- In terms of the drivers: 

   - LTM EBITDA grew by 10.7% y/y,  at a similar pace as the EBITDA growth rate for the HY market. 

   - Total debt grew at a slower pace – 6.4% y/y in 4Q18. However, total debt still grew at a much faster pace vs that of the HY market (1.8%), as leveraged borrowers increasingly chose to fund deals in the loan market. 

   - Additionally, 3M Libor increased by 41bp in 4Q18 (with LIBOR-OIS rising by 22bp), and repricings fell from $233bn in 1H18 to $37bn in 2H18. 

- **With the Fed pausing its rate hiking cycle, the headwind of rising LIBOR levels may subside for now. In fact, so far in 2019 to-date, LIBOR is ~20bp lower.** However, with most of the loan market trading below par, repricings activity should remain muted, and as discussed in the HY section above, we expect much slower EBITDA growth in 2019. Hence, we think it is likely that interest coverage will again start declining. 

- **Loan balance sheet liquidity deteriorated for the fourth straight quarter.** Loan cash/debt is now at 7.34%, the lowest level since 2013. Cash/debt is lower y/y and q/q and below the long-term median of 8.1%.
