**Implied vols – how low can they go?** Not much.  A combination of factors has contributed to the steady decline in gamma since the middle of this year (Exhibit 3). The main driver once again is the Fed on-hold and range-bound rates causing a steady decline in realized volatility. In recent weeks however, we have seen a pickup in supply of gamma from the swapping of callable issuance and other yield enhancement strategies which have been pressuring implied vols lower. In gamma space, implied vols are now trading in line with 1m realized vols (Exhibit 4). The demand for vol on the other hand has been petering out, with mortgage hedgers mostly on the sidelines and some demand from leveraged accounts to buy protection against a rise in rates. With limited demand for vol and a marginal increase in the supply of vol, we expect vols to remain under pressure until year-end. 

**TIPS breakevens to stay wide:** TIPS breakevens have had an impressive run since the beginning of October. 10y breakevens at 217bp are near the highest level since the beginning of this year and not far from the average level of 250bp maintained from 2003 to June 2008 **,** prior to the onset of the financial crisis. The correlation between oil prices and 10y TIPS breakevens has made a comeback.  In mid October however, 10y TIPS breakevens lagged the rise in oil prices. But that quickly changed after the Fed reaffirmed its commitment to keeping rates low for an extended period of time in last week’s meeting (see _FOMC Statement Aftermath: Higher Inflation Risk Premiums_ published on November 4). Breakevens are now much more in line with gold and oil prices (Exhibits 5 and 6). With oil at close to $80 and gold at a historically high $1,100, inflation concerns are now front and center.  Wide breakevens are probably here to stay. 

In conclusion, we recommend reducing risks with the expectation that current trading ranges will prevail until year-end. We propose trade ideas that would benefit given reduced risk appetites and range-bound markets over the near term.

## **$21 Billion Mid-November Coupon Payment Favors a UST 2s10s Flattener** 

With the week’s Treasury supply out of the way, the next important date to keep in mind is November 16 (Monday), when Treasury investors will get back $21 billion in coupon payments from bonds with an average maturity of 10 years (Exhibit 1 - top). This cash flow will thus have to be reinvested roughly around the 10y point. 

Since 2000, this has created a flattening seasonal bias (Exhibit 1 - bottom), which favors a 2s10s flattener from now to Tuesday close. Over the past nine years, the average three-day flattening centered on the coupon date generated a 6bp return on the 2s10s curve (flattening 6x, steepening 2x, flat 1x over the past nine years). 

While we continue to hold a core steepener view (see _FOMC Statement Aftermath: Higher Inflation Risk Premiums_ , November 4, for our latest thoughts), we initiate a 3- day tactical 2s10s Treasury flattener to take advantage of this strong seasonality. At 264bp, the 2s10s Treasury curve is also at its multi-month highs, which presents us with an excellent entry point for this tactical flattener trade. The trade does lose 11bp in rolldown and carry over a 3M period, but given our short horizon, this is not critical. 

The risk to this trade is that our core view bears out even through the November 16 coupon payment date and the 2s10s curve continues to steepen. 

## **Taking Profits on Our Long 2s5s10s UST Butterfly, Underweight the Belly** 

In our prior weekly publication, _The Deafening Silence of the Yield Curve,_ published on September 17, we recommended purchasing 5y Treasuries on a 2s5s10s butterfly, which was trading at the cheap end of its 3-month range versus the level of rates. We suggested underweighting the belly by regression-based 25% of the risk in the wings (causing the fly to trade net short) to neutralize this fly versus the level of rates – which otherwise explain nearly 80% of its moves – and play for a richening of the 5y on the curve with minimal duration or curve risk. 

This week we hit our PNL target of making 3bps with this trade and look to cover now that 10y supply is out of the way and 5y supply is looming come November 24. While the 2s5s10s butterfly is still very cheap on a historical basis at 13bp (belly – ½ * sum of wings), it is now trading at the rich end versus the level of rates (Exhibit 2). 

## **…and Although We Think 5s Are Rich, Fading Them Is Risky** 

While we do think that the 5y point has richened considerably both on the curve and to the level of rates, which would seem to suggest that we should now go the other way and sell 5s on 2s5s10s (again underweight the belly to hedge the rates directionality), we are less confident in selling 5s on the curve. The reason is that we are implicitly short the option that 5s continue to richen to pre-June 2009 levels of 0bp.

CMO demand has been a strong driver of demand for agency jumbos. CMOs have bought 35% of the outstanding in 4.5s, 5s and 5.5s, compared to 30% for the same coupons in TBA Golds (traditionally considered very driven by CMO demand). CMO production is focusing on 4.5s and 5s right now as lower dollar prices allow for more attractively priced CMOs. As a result, we have seen valuations on those jumbo coupons being put into CMOs, 5s and especially 4.5s, tighten close to model valuations, while 5.5s still trade cheap to the model. Therefore, we recommend that any investor holding jumbos in pass-through form move up-in-coupon, giving the CMO machine the bonds it has a bid for and moving into cheaper bonds to be compensated for their negative convexity. 

## **Why Do Jumbos Trade Cheaper than TBAs to Begin With?** 

Much of the CMO demand is driven by the fact that jumbos trade cheaper (in price terms) than TBAs. There are two major reasons for this: 

1. **Higher Loan Sizes and Better Credit Quality** : Jumbos, as the name implies, have higher loan sizes than TBAs do. Higher loan sizes make it easier for borrowers to cover the fixed costs of refinancing, making it more likely that borrowers will take advantage of lower rates. Furthermore, jumbos also have higher credit scores and lower LTVs, making it even more likely that borrowers will refinance. Indeed, we have seen much higher speed on Jumbos than TBAs in the current low rate environment (Exhibit 2). However, loan sizes on TBA mortgages are rising with home prices, so as the bonds season, sizes should be less and less of a factor. Similarly, tighter underwriting standards should push credit scores and LTVs on TBAs closer to those on jumbos. 

2. **Liquidity** : Jumbos are not deliverable into TBA contracts, and the market should extract a liquidity premium. With all the liquidity currently in the system, Jumbos are trading close to their theoretical values (not pricing in any liquidity premium for TBAs at all). As the Fed removes liquidity from the system, we would expect this trend to reverse. 

## **What About CMOs?** 

The demand from the CMO machine appears to be driven by demand for front sequentials, with the result that the front sequentials are trading much richer than the last cash flows. Front sequentials are the first cash flows in the CMO; with a modified duration of just over 3 years and a nominal yield of Libor + 181, they are attractive bonds from a rolldown and carry perspective. However, in exchange for
