## **European Economics Weekly | Europe** 

## Bank Funding Focus 

The ECB should remain dovish and may even announce TLTRO replacement, though April looks perhaps more likely. Euro area data will probably stabilise, but growth isn't set to recover yet. In the UK, we expect less Brexit news ahead of the next key vote, and further weak survey data. 

**Euro Area | A cautious ECB message:** Discussion around **TLTRO replacement** could gain more solid grounding at Thursday's central bank meeting. With the **staff projections,** for both inflation and growth, set to be **revised down significantly,** this month or April provide opportunities for the ECB to act on bank funding. In many ways, though, these forecast revisions are a look-back, reflecting softer data in late 2018. In fact, we expect upcoming hard data to tentatively point to **some stabilisation in the economy early this year.** Euro area **retail sales** likely bounced back from a weak December and we expect **industrial production** to rise in the largest countries. That's not to say that growth is reaccelerating – our base case is that it won't recover until later this year. But the data may offer signs that the outlook is no longer getting incrementally worse. 

**UK | Building to the second meaningful vote (MV#2):** After confirmation that MV#2 will be held by **March 12,** we'll be watching for any news from the **Irish backstop negotiations.** However, we expect the outcome of the talks – in our view, a protocol on the temporary nature of the backstop, but no time limit or unilateral exit clause, and a legal opinion from the Attorney General agreeing that the backstop should be temporary – not to be published until just before MV#2. On the data front, we expect the **PMI services** to recover modestly, but to still suggest economic weakness amid political uncertainty. We're forecasting a **below-consensus 0.1%Q** expansion in 1Q, lower than the MPC's expectations.
