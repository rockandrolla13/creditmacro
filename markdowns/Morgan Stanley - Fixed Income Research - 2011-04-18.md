## **High Yield Fundamentals: Pricing Tomorrow’s Deleveraging Today** 

**European high yield fundamentals suggest a normal cycle:** Across the 94 high yield issuers that we track, credit quality consolidated gains in 4Q10. Interest coverage and cash flow metrics improved quarter on quarter, though deleveraging progress paused. 

**Expect more dispersion mid-cycle:** Margins may have peaked, and a strong euro and the rate cycle should drive dispersion in high yield operating performance. In addition, we’re watching M&A activity, more aggressive financing and the rebuilding capital stock. 

**Expect relative underperformance:** While we maintain our equal-weight on high yield, we expect it to underperform investment grade. High yield has outperformed recently, and valuations may be tested. We like selling Main and buying XOver protection in a 4:1 ratio. 

**Screens and investment ideas:** We screen for bonds with favourable spread-adjusted valuations, solid free cash flow and larger margins of error on interest expense. We contrast these to bonds meeting less favourable criteria. We also position for decorrelation in the tranche market, and identify opportunities for spread pick-up between CDS and bonds.

## **High Yield – More Disperse Outcomes Going Forward** 

- As ‘unusual’ as the last three years have been, many balance sheet trends suggest a surprisingly normal credit cycle. European high yield, the focus of this week’s note, is no exception. 

- The breadth of leverage improvement remained strong. Over 70% of high yield issuers reduced leveraged YoY in 4Q10, a level that has recently marked the peak of market breadth. Although we would expect moderate improvement to credit quality going forward, we expect a slow pace and wider variance. 

- At bottom left we show the YTD changes for Crossover constituents relative to the index.  Compared to the same period last year, performance is more dispersed, a healthy sign that the current market is willing to differentiate between different credit stories.

## **HY Fundamentals: Interest Coverage and Liquidity** 

- Median interest coverage, defined as EBITDA/interest expense, rebounded from an anaemic 2.8x to 3.4x, close to December 2003 levels. Despite this improvement, coverage remains well below pre-crisis levels, despite the low level of rates. Our economists’ expectation for Euribor to rise to 2% by the end of 2011, as well as a continued trend of refinancing floating-rate loan debt into higher-coupon fixed-rate bonds, provide headwinds to improvement. 

- Across investment grade and high yield issuers, European corporates have been able to maintain an impressive liquidity profile in recent quarters. HY FCF to total debt stood at 8.6% at year-end, and cash balances added up to 21.5% of total debt.

## **Re lenishin the Ca ital Stock Will Pressure Cash p g p** 

- Cash generation has often come at the expense of capital expenditures. Bottom left shows LTM capex relative to LTM depreciation expense, where the ratio has fallen from 102% at the end of 2008 to 63% in 2Q10, and has rebounded to 79% at year-end. Given the level of cuts seen during the crisis, we believe it is reasonable to expect a large ‘payback’ as companies begin to reinvest. 

- More ‘macro’ data from the ECB would seem to confirm this. Plotting bank lending survey data at bottom right, demand for loans towards working capital and capex are recovering strongly. This may reflect increased confidence in investment and inventories, but such spending may hurt free cash flow generation in the near term.

## **The Valuation Picture** 

- At a current level of 132bp/x, European high yield pays investors one of the lowest levels for each turn of the balance sheet since 2003, in contrast to the investment grade market, which is slightly above average. With a view that the market will shift its focus from systemic fears towards the ‘mid-cycle’ challenges for rising rates and higher input costs, we see investment grade benefiting more from the former and high yield more exposed to the latter, with valuation providing further support. 

- A risk to this view is that today’s market looks surprisingly similar to December 2003, a period that saw continued strength in performance. While we acknowledge the risk, we believe that several factors are different at present: The ECB is hiking now, while in the last cycle it didn’t start raising rates until 2005. The euro is at 1.45 today, versus 1.25 to start 2004. Senior bank spreads are significantly higher, and fiscal policy is restrictive, rather than expansionary.

## **Other Ways to Play High Yield** 

- **Selling bonds, selling CDS:** The divergence between cash bonds and CDS at the single-name level is a good way to improve alpha. At bottom left, we identify a number of bonds with positive basis, where it pays to sell the bond and instead take risk through CDS. 

- **Position for decompression:** High yield’s greater sensitivity to a softer European economy, combined with its recent outperformance, leads us to favour decompression trades today. Using indices (which are an admittedly blunt instrument), we’d prefer to express this as selling Main protection, buying XOver protection, on a 4:1 ratio (i.e., slightly long beta/positive carry).
