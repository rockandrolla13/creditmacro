## EU Credit | **Glass Half Full** 

**Base case:** Geopolitical uncertainty, significant supply, and our US colleagues’ view of wider US spreads limit the beta upside. We see EUR IG spreads settling at 80bp leading to ~0.7% excess returns. 

We lifted our earlier EUR HY spread target to 300bp, as slowing growth and more restrictive policy put greater pressure on distressed tails and default rates. Loans outperform supported by rising base rates and wider risk premiums. 

**Bull case:** Convincing evidence of a fast reopening of the Strait of Hormuz would be a ‘mild’ bull case, although with valuations now close to the pre-conflict levels, the long-term impact would be more limited. For our bull case scenario, stronger consumer demand, declining energy prices lift growth without sapping markets of attractive yields. This scenario takes us closer to a 2006-style converge towards cycle tights.

## Asia Credit | **Tight Spreads Are the Result of An Uneasy Equilibrium** 

- At first glance, year-to-date outflows from Asia credit funds should have put greater widening pressure on Asia IG spreads. 

- However, Asia credit net supply have been significantly negative over the same period, which has helped offset the impact of fund outflows. 

- We believe the above helps explain the current tight level of Asia IG spread, which is clearly pricing in our de-escalation scenario for the Strait of Hormuz. The widest Asia IG spread traded this year was at 72bps, 7bps wider that start of the year. 

- Much like the Fed’s “low-fire, low-hire” labor market, which reflects an uneasy equilibrium, Asia credit investors are facing a similar predicament in a “negative net supply, negative fund flows” asset class.

- According to the Merton Model, a company’s equity is a call option on its total assets, with the strike price equal to the face value of its debt due at maturity. 

- A higher equity valuation for a company tends to imply that the company’s asset value has increased.

## **Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO)** 

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO's original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid.
