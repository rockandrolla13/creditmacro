## Global growth momentum starts to recover: what is priced in? 

**Global growth momentum starts to recover following the H2 slowdown:** PMIs in all three major regions – China, the US and the Euro area – rose in February, leading the global PMI to increase by around 1 point, the first improvement since August. The rise was particularly strong in China, where the manufacturing PMI rebounded by 3 points, the largest monthly gain since 2013. Going forward, we still see around 3 points upside for the Euro area PMI (driven by a fade in temporary drags, the recent EUR depreciation and a more favourable inventory cycle) and a further 1-point rise in the China PMI (driven by a continued improvement in the China credit impulse on the back of the recent cuts in the reserve requirement ratio), with this partly offset by moderate downside for the US PMI (given the drag from past USD strength). Overall, these projections are consistent with a further 0.5 point increase in the global PMI by early Q2. 

## **What is priced in?** 

- **European equities have overshot fair value on our model, but we remain neutral on the market:** The Stoxx 600 has risen from being meaningfully below the fair-value level implied by our model (based on Euro area PMIs, the EUR and Euro area real bond yields) in December to being 8% above fair value. Even if PMIs continue to improve in line with our projections, our model points to downside for the Stoxx 600 over the coming months. Yet, we remain neutral on the market, as we see the risk that a fade in political risk (on the back of a US / China trade truce and a move towards a Brexit deal) leads to a continued overshoot. 

- **We keep our long-standing underweight in Europe versus US:** European equities have outperformed US equities by 6% since the low in early December last year, against our expectation of continued underperformance. We retain our long-held underweight of Europe versus the US given that: (a) while the Stoxx 600 has already overshot our model-implied fair value, the S&P 500 is still 5% below fair value on our model (based on the global PMI and the broad USD trade-weighted index). Our macro projections suggest European equities are set to underperform US equities by 10% over the coming months; (b) Europe tends to outperform the US only when the global PMI is above 55, while the current level of 52.5 in combination with our expectation of only a mild further rise is consistent with renewed underperformance for European equities.

## Global growth momentum starts to recover: what is priced in? 

## **What is priced in?** 

**Bond yields are not yet priced for an improvement in growth momentum:** This asset class has not yet moved even as equities have rallied and cyclicals have outperformed. Our rates strategists have recently turned bearish on Bunds. We agree, with our model (based on Euro area PMIs, Euro area core inflation, Brent and global central bank balance sheets) pointing to upside for Bund yields to 0.6% by Q3. Our US 10-year bond yield model (based on global PMIs, US inflation, Brent and global central bank balance sheets) suggests US bond yields are already 2030bps below fair value and should have upside to 3.2% by mid-Q2. 

- **We see further upside for European cyclicals versus defensives:** Our index of European cyclicals (which excludes financials and resources) has outperformed defensives by 6% since the start of the year, reducing the gap to the model-implied fair value that had opened up in Q4 last year. We remain overweight cyclicals versus defensives, given our projections of: (a) US 10-year bond yields rising to 3.2% in combination with 3% downside for the USD by mid-Q3 (on the back of a normalisation in global policy uncertainty), which should weigh on European defensives; and (b) a recovery in Euro area PMIs over the coming months and only mild further CNY weakness against the EUR (as implied by our China economists’ more positive view on the CNY), both of which should help cyclicals. Taken together, our macro expectations suggest cyclicals are set to outperform defensives by a further 9% by Q3. 

**We remain overweight European banks and underweight most European bond yield proxies:** Our expectations of higher Bund yields and Euro area PMIs imply 15% upside for European banks’ price relative by late Q3. As a consequence, we remain overweight banks, but note that the weakness in Euro area PMIs earlier this year still implies near-term downside risk for the sector. We remain underweight real estate (the key German bond proxy in the European sector space) and stay underweight utilities (which also tend to move inversely to Bund yields). Our expectation for US 10-year yields to rise to 3.2% by mid-Q2 underpins our underweights in pharma, telecoms and Swiss equities. It also implies downside for food & beverages, following the recent outperformance. As a consequence, we downgrade the sector from benchmark to underweight (see below). We stay overweight personal & household goods; however, given that the sector has upside on our model even if US bond yields rise in line with our projections.

## Global growth momentum starts to recover: what is priced in? 

**What is priced in?** 

**We downgrade European food & beverages from benchmark to underweight:** we upgraded food & beverages from underweight to benchmark in late January after 5% underperformance since mid-December, which caused the sector to close the overshot to fair value that had opened up during the growth scare in late Q4. Since then, it has outperformed by 7% as the US 10-year bond yield – the key driver of its relative performance – has faded by a further 15bps. We now downgrade food & beverages, given our US bond yield projections imply 7% underperformance by July. 

**We downgrade European consumer durables from benchmark to underweight:** consumer durables (which is mostly luxury goods) typically benefits when global PMI momentum is improving, but 7% outperformance since the start of the year leaves it priced for a stronger rise in global PMIs than we expect. While consumer durables can outperform its PMI-implied trajectory when the CNY strengthens against the EUR (since the sector has one of the highest sales exposures to emerging markets), our China economists’ CNY forecast still implies 3% downside for the CNY against the EUR over the coming six months. Taken together, our PMI and FX projections imply 8% downside for consumer durables’ price relative by late Q2, leading us to downgrade the sector from benchmark to underweight.
