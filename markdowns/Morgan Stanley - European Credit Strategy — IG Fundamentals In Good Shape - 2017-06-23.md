## **European Credit Strategy** 

## IG Fundamentals: In Good Shape 

1Q17 financial statements point to continued divergence between European and US corporate behaviour. Earnings momentum has far outpaced debt growth in Europe, resulting in lower leverage and higher cash balances across most sectors. 

**Corporate balance sheets continue to strengthen:** Despite the improved earnings backdrop and availability of cheap funding, European corporates have shown remarkable restraint. Gross and net leverage declined in 1Q17, reaching 2.8x and 1.8x, respectively. The decline is broad-based, in terms of sectors and the size of weaker tails. The data reinforce the deleveraging trend that has been in place for the past three quarters. 

**Liquidity metrics remain historically elevated:** While there was a slight decline in median balance sheet cash/debt compared to 4Q16, it is still close to the record high of 30% seen in early 2014. More encouragingly, the distribution of our universe improved year on year, with almost 40% of companies now recording cash/debt in excess of 40%. Median interest coverage ratios also improved to new highs on the back of stronger earnings. 

**Reverse Yankee fundamentals:** The growing presence of US issuers in European credit markets makes their fundamentals increasingly important for investors in the region. We note that median issuer net leverage has risen substantially in recent years, closely mirroring the trends seen in the broader US IG market. Their re-leveraging activity stands in stark contrast to the conservative behaviour of European corporates. 

**Telecoms screens favourably in our sector framework:** The sector's high ranking is helped by relatively better fundamental trends over the last year and more attractive valuations. At the opposite end of the spectrum, media and consumer discretionary now screen as the least attractive sectors on account of weaker fundamental trends and below-average valuations. Utilities also continue to fare poorly, primarily as the sector looks least attractive from a valuation perspective. 

**Screening for single-name value:** Based on the updated fundamentals, we screen for corporates that score well on both valuation and fundamental metrics, as well as names with the opposite characteristics.

## In good shape 

We have spent the last two weeks meeting a broad variety of investors to discuss our mid-year outlook (see Less to Play For, June 9, 2017). Based on the feedback, it is clear that we remain more bearish versus consensus, although the deviation would appear to be less than it was at the beginning of the year. While investors remain wary of the concerns flagged by us – central bank balance sheets and the risk/reward in a late-cycle environment – a majority still view this as an environment where credit spreads continue to move sideways for longer. Balance sheet concerns in the US, which our colleagues have been highlighting for some time (see 1Q17 US Credit Fundamental Review, June 9, 2017), are also well acknowledged but countered by the argument that the catalysts exposing these weak balance sheets are less obvious for now. 

Closer to home, European corporate behaviour remains at odds with their US counterparts. Balance sheet trends are less concerning as the strong earnings impulse translates into lower corporate leverage. As this report details, corporate fundamentals have continued to improve in 1Q17, with falling leverage, rising cash balances and higher interest coverage ratios. This discipline against a seemingly ideal backdrop for expansionary corporate behaviour has been remarkable. Low rates, tight spreads, improving earnings combined with a central bank directly purchasing corporate bonds should eventually push the cycle ahead in Europe. While this may still be some way off, we reiterate that, in a global cycle, the weakest links provide the early clues. US fundamentals and market technicals are factors that concern us more than European fundamentals. Moreover, with reverse Yankees accounting for 18% of the EUR IG market, we caution that investors here are also exposed to the US fundamental narrative. 

## Leverage declines further as earnings growth accelerates 

As usual, we start our analysis of corporate fundamentals by examining leverage trends for the median European corporate (Exhibit 3). Gross leverage decreased by 0.2x year on year in 1Q17, reaching 2.8x. Net leverage fell by a similar amount year on year and now stands at 1.8x, close to the post-crisis low of 1.6x seen in early 2011. These developments keep in place the deleveraging trend that began a year ago. 

In order to have a deeper understanding of what is causing the moves in leverage, we track how the numerator and denominator have evolved over time. Exhibit 4 plots median total debt and EBITDA growth and shows that earnings momentum has continued to strengthen while debt growth has moderated. More specifically, the median corporate saw last 12-month EBITDA increase by 6.6%Y in 1Q17, compared to 5.3%Y in the prior quarter. At the same time, total debt rose by only 1.4%Y, slightly lower than the already muted pace of 1.7%Y in 4Q16.

Our equity strategy colleagues expect the trend of improving earnings to continue, at least for the remainder of 2017, though 2018 is likely to be more challenging (see Curbing Our Enthusiasm, June 4, 2017). In the interim, we reiterate our view that the pickup in earnings should provide corporates with more debt capacity without hurting their leverage metrics. Ultimately, the combination of improved economic growth and the ECB's commitment to continue corporate bond purchases through to the end of the year should result in the European credit cycle progressing, though there are limited signs of this so far.

## Cash/debt remains elevated 

Beyond the improvements seen on the leverage front, liquidity metrics also appear healthy. Balance sheet cash/debt for the median corporate stands at 28% as of 1Q17, marginally down QoQ but well above the 23% seen a year earlier (Exhibit 7). Much like the leverage numbers, the improvement in the median cash level appears to be driven by a broad improvement. In Exhibit 8 we compare the distribution of our universe across the different cash/debt buckets in 1Q17 to a year earlier. On this measure it appears that the stronger cohorts have all increased in size, while most of the weaker cohorts have shrunk. Overall, the portion of the universe with cash/debt above 30% has increased by 7pp, mirrored by a decline in all other buckets.

## Interest coverage hits new record-highs 

Unsurprisingly, the accelerating earnings momentum highlighted earlier and the continued low yield environment have led to a further improvement in interest coverage ratios (ICR). Year on year, ICR rose by 0.7x in 1Q17, touching a new high of 6.0x (Exhibit 10). In Exhibit 11 we show how year-on-year changes in ICR fare against leverage at the single-name level. There was a marked increased in the share of firms seeing a rise in ICR, going from 54% in 1Q16 to 69% in 1Q17. In fact, more than half of the firms in our fundamental sample reported positive ICR growth and declining net leverage. On the flip side, 20% of companies saw both metrics deteriorate, down from 35% a year earlier.

**Why did ICR rise?** In order to answer this question, we present the median growth of its components in Exhibit 12. Modest debt growth at a time when companies can still benefit by refinancing maturing bonds at lower yields than at issue meant that YoY interest expense remained in negative territory. The combined effect of falling interest expenses amid very low yields and improving earnings momentum contributed to the recent upswing in ICR.

## Reverse Yankee fundamental trends less encouraging 

Given the strong reverse Yankee issuance in recent years, European credit fundamentals are no longer sufficient to gauge the EUR-denominated credit market's health. As we show in Exhibit 16, US corporates make up nearly a fifth of the EUR IG non-financial universe, a figure that has almost tripled since 2013. In order to develop a more comprehensive picture of the fundamental risks that EUR credit investors are exposed to, we compare net leverage for the median reverse Yankee issuer to the median European and US IG corporate in Exhibit 17. Here, we work with a sample of 71 US issuers currently in the iBoxx EUR IG index and calculate the median leverage for these companies based on data from our US colleagues. It should come as no surprise that the recent trend for these issuers reflects the re-leveraging in the US market and contrasts with the discussion above on European corporates. In terms of absolute levels, the US sample has converged with European issuers despite a far more benign starting point in 2014. Net leverage for the median reverse Yankee issuer stood at 1.9x as of 1Q17, having nearly doubled from 2011. The overall level remains well above the previous cycle peaks and is now above the broader EU IG net leverage.
