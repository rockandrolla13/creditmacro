## What Do Recoveries Usually Look Like? 

We examine cross-asset sequencing from prior bear market lows, and what would be most and least unusual if March 23 was the bottom. We reiterate our OW in credit. 

**An unusual bear market – an unusual recovery:** The recent sell-off in global equities has been an outlier in speed and severity. So has the rebound, in terms of the duration, magnitude and sector leadership from the lows. 

**FAQ 1: How long could a recovery take?** Global equities typically takes 15 months to recover to prior peaks after hitting bear market lows. V-shaped market recoveries are historically rare. 

**FAQ 2: How much can global equities bounce back?** On median, ACWI rallies 35% in the 12 months after a trough. While there's _some_ relationship between depth of sell-off and strength of subsequent recovery, it's weaker than one may think. 

## **FAQ 3: How do sectors behave around equity market troughs?** Global 

defensives outperform going into bear market troughs and underperform as equities rally, consistent with US market history as noted by the US equity strategy team, and what's occurred this sell-off.

## **The other factor to consider in terms of the severity and recovery of this bear market is** 

**recession risk:** Our economists now expect the dislocation and disruption from COVID19 to trigger a US and global recession this year, with global growth for 2020 dipping to -0.6%Y, the lowest since the global financial crisis. This is _despite_ the aggressive policy support we touched on above, as they see that the underlying damage from both COVID-19’s impact and tighter financial conditions will deliver a material shock to the global economy. 

Focusing on the US where we have fuller history, bear markets which saw recessions begin within 12 months after equities peak have historically been more severe than those not associated with growth contractions – the median drawdown was 48% compared to 34% for non-recessionary bear markets, with a duration of ~1.5 years versus 6-7 months for the latter. **If our economists are correct in their forecast for a recession, and if March 23 was indeed the market bottom, US equities' 34% peak-totrough slide would be one of the** _**milder**_ **bear markets associated with a recession.**

## FAQ 1: How long could a recovery take? 

## **On median, MSCI ACWI took 15 months to recover to prior peaks after troughing in a** 

**bear market;** for S&P 500, MSCI Europe and TOPIX, recovery to prior peaks typically took 20 months. However, as Exhibit 8 shows, there's a very wide range around duration of recovery. For example, after the 1998 sell-off, MSCI ACWI bounced back within two months but took more than _five years_ to climb back to prior peaks after the GFC. For the likes of MSCI EM, we are _still_ below 2007 heights. And for those who have high hopes for a quick rebound, a sobering factoid: **V-shaped market recoveries are rare –** of the 17 episodes of 20%+ declines in S&P 500 we've identified going back to 1929, only one (1932/33) saw the index recover to prior peaks within a year.

## FAQ 4: How do assets behave around equity market troughs? 

Exhibit 22 summarises the median performance of various markets around ACWI troughs. What's notable: 

- **Government bonds:** UST 10Y and Bunds 10Y tend to see yields decline into global equity troughs, with most of the rally coming in the six months before stocks bottom. After bear market troughs, yields on median rise for six months, before declining once more. The US 2s10s yield curve tends to flatten into equity troughs, and steepen after. If March 23 was the equity market bottom, yields have fallen more and curves have steepened more than what's usually experienced around troughs. 

- **Credit:** Both IG and HY credit spreads widen into stock bottoms, with the most severe widening on median occurring in the month right before bear market troughs. In the 12 months after ACWI makes lows, credit tightens materially – by 130bp for US IG and 500bp for US HY. US credit performance around the March 23 trough has actually been in line with the prior bear market experience, but EUR HY has widened out less. 

- **FX:** DXY strengthens mildly into ACWI troughs, and then weakens by about 2% on median in the 12 months after. JPY typically strengthens into _and_ out of global equity bottoms. DXY has actually weakened more going into the March 23 trough 

6 

than usual, while JPY and EUR have gone into the equity rebound stronger than what one would expect. 

- **Commodities:** Broad commodities generally weakens into a bear market trough, and then strengthens out of it, but this belies the fact that WTI and gold tend to start seeing positive returns in advance of stocks making a bottom. Given the extreme sell-off we had seen in oil, it comes as no surprise that WTI looks the most out-of-line with what one tends to see around equity troughs. 

- **Volatility:** VIX and V2X both spike by about 20 points in the six-month run-up into an equity bottom, and then normalise in the 12 months after. In the latest sell-off, VIX has risen much more around the March 23 equity trough than historical bear trough episodes would suggest.

## FAQ 5: What could the sequencing of a 'rolling recovery' look like? 

Just as we made generalisations about market sequencing around equity peaks and the 'rolling bear market' (see Cross-Asset Dispatches: A Spotter's Guide to Bull Corrections and Bear Markets, Revisited (21 Oct 2018), we can also make an educated guess to what a 'rolling recovery' may look like that may signal an end to this bear market. 

In Exhibit 23 and Exhibit 24, we show when the median trend for various assets and US macro indicators have peaked and troughed in +/-12 months around ACWI and S&P 500 troughs – in effect, they are checklists of which markets to get more constructive on and when. We'll focus on the US list, given that MSCI ACWI and S&P 500 typically trough on the same day. A generalised sequencing: 

- **Six months before equity troughs:** Bond yields peak, and the yield curve starts to steepen; around the same time, the Fed starts to cut rates and gold starts to rally. 

- **Three months before equity troughs:** Implied volatility peaks, followed by credit spreads peaking, commodities rebounding and EM credit spreads peaking. 

- **One week before equity troughs:** US consumer sentiment troughs, materials begins to outperform.

Markets have seldom waited for data to get better before pricing in a rosier growth outlook, consistent with our finding that equities tend to bottom 4-5 months _before_ the economic cycle does.
