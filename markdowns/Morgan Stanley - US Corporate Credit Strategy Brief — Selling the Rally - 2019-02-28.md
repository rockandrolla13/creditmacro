## **US Corporate Credit Strategy Brief | North America** 

## Selling the Rally 

On January 7, 2019, we turned tactically constructive on US credit (see US Corporate Credit Strategy Brief: Finally Fair, 2019.01.07). **In our view, the trade is now mostly done and the risk/reward of remaining meaningfully long US credit is no longer attractive.** Specifically, we are exiting our tactical buy recommendation, and reverting to our longer-term cautious view on the asset class. 

Our short-term bullish view was based on two simple points. **One, we believed sentiment had shifted too far in the negative direction at the end of 2018** , when recession fears were rising fast, many had started to believe the Fed was making a policy mistake, and to some investors, our expectation for spreads to trend wider this year, in fact, didn’t seem bearish enough. **Two, markets finally moved**

Then why have markets bounced so aggressively? While there are likely other explanations, the dovish Fed shift combined with the expectation for a China trade deal would be high on the list. And without a doubt, both of these factors were short-term positives, especially coming on the back of such an oversold market late last year. But neither change our view on the broader credit cycle. 

## **With respect to a more dovish Fed, don’t forget that the bulk of spread widening in a cycle actually happens AFTER the hiking cycle is done, because** 

**that is when growth weakens most.** The sequence Fed policy follows as the cycle turns is that monetary policy first becomes tight and the Treasury curve flattens, then usually inverts. Eventually, the Fed stops tightening. As growth keeps slowing (and/or financial conditions keep tightening), the Fed cuts and the Treasury curve bull steepens into a recession. Hence, somewhat counterintuitively, if the Fed has finished hiking for the cycle, as rates markets could be suggesting, that may make it MORE likely that a bigger turn in the credit cycle is near, given this typical sequence, and what it implies about the trajectory of the US economy. 

**And tracking the various stages of a turn in the credit cycle as we laid out in our 2019 US Credit Outlook: The Bear Has Begun, 2018.11.25, we think the boxes** 

**continue to get checked,** with the tightening in bank lending conditions across C&I, CRE and consumer loans in the last Fed Senior Loan Officer Survey as just one of the most recent examples.

What could drive the next move wider in spreads? Global macro data have been weak over the past few months across a wide range of indicators, while our equity strategists have pointed out that 1Q19 may mark the beginning of an outright earnings recession (see: US Equity Strategy: Weekly Warm Up: Earnings Recession Is Here, 2019.02.11). In our view, the next big move lower in risk may come if, DESPITE a dovish Fed and a potential agreement on trade, the macro data and/or earnings expectations simply keep weakening. **Investors may then worry that the problems in 4Q, in fact, were not just about the Fed or trade, but** 

## **potentially about a late-cycle economy that is starting to show bigger cracks.**

## **Of course, we could be wrong, and markets may be telling us that the global economic data are about to trough. Even if that is the case, we think upside is** 

**limited.** Remember, unlike during past growth slowdowns earlier in the cycle, the Fed does not have the luxury of significant slack in labor markets and inflation well below target. That means if growth starts reaccelerating, and financial conditions keep easing, the market may have to quickly price rate hikes back in. And let’s not forget the headwinds from tighter Fed policy have not fully disappeared, just because of the dovish Fed pivot. The balance sheet is still shrinking for now and prior rate hikes are likely still filtering through, while global central banks in many cases are still adding less liquidity. 

## **Regardless of the exact catalyst, in early January, investors were paid to take the position that the next bout of weakness in markets was several months or more away. They aren’t anymore.** 

In terms of positioning, we recommend the following: 

**First, we think now is a good time to short the liquid derivatives indices.** While 

we believe cash will underperform CDX longer term, in past selloffs, the liquid

(The risks to this trade are that vol remains low and spreads continue to grind tighter, or the liquidity premium again increases so IG CDX outperforms on the way down.)

**Second, we shift back into loans relative to high yield.** Earlier this year, we made the opposite switch, from loans into HY. We thought the HY market looked cheaper than loans after the 4Q selloff, and we expected technicals to remain relatively weaker in the loan market, on top of a more challenging long-term fundamental story for loans. While we remain more concerned about some of the technical and fundamental challenges specific to the loan market, the 4Q18 selloff should have reminded investors that HY is still the higher-beta market, with HY falling by over seven points, while loans declined by less than five. And given our call for wider spreads, we think it makes sense to be underweight beta in portfolios. (The risk is that, if spreads move sideways and/or tighten while Treasury yields fall, HY may outperform loans.)
