## **Trade Idea:** 

## • **Buy the Dec Gilt basis, using a 50% weight on the 2018 as well as 50% on the 2021** 

Unlike the Sep Gilt contract, the Dec contract has two issues vying to be the CTD, and there is a high probability that either could end up as the final CTD (Exhibit 1). 

We believe the market is underestimating the optionality of a CTD switch, and would recommend buying the basis, using a 50% weight on the 2018 as well as 50% on the 2021. We believe this offers a cheap way to be long gamma. 

The significant difference in dv01 between the front end and back end of the basket will have important implications for trading and hedging in the Dec contract, as well as the Mar 09 contract eventually, which may also experience similar optionality.

## **Optionality Forgotten** 

The 8.75% of 2017 has been the undisputed CTD into Gilt future contracts since the G M7. Even prior to this, the 8% of 2015 ruled. This has some important implications: 

- The switch option has been next to worthless for a number of years 

- The Dec 06 contract was the last time there was any hint of optionality, and even then the switch probability was low and worth only 3 months of duration 

- The high coupon on the 2015s and 2017s meant that it was more attractive to earn carry during the delivery month and deliver late 

We use a 2-factor PCA model to forecast the behaviour of issues in the delivery basket under various yield curve scenarios. We estimate that the probability of CTD is split evenly at 50% each for the 2018 and 2021 issues, assuming late delivery (split is about 60:40 if we assume early delivery). The converted dv01 for the Futures contract changes from about 8.5 to 9.7 in a CTD switch, which has significant hedging implications. For example, in a parallel sell-off, the 2021 would cheapen more than the 2018, given its higher dv01, and hence the Future would need to re-price to track the new CTD. This means that a dv01 neutral basis position using the 2018s would need substantial rehedging. The converse would also be true for a basis position using the 2021s. 

The 2018 net basis has been trading around 0-2 cents during the roll period, and widened on Thursday to close at 8.8 cents. However, this would widen significantly more if the CTD were to switch to the 2021. 

This, though, is not the full story. If the 2018s remain as CTD, the current repo of around 5.12% makes holding the issue a negative carry position – which would encourage early delivery – though a rate cut by the BoE could take the repo rate below the 5% coupon, switching carry to negative during the delivery month and hence reverting to late delivery. On the other hand, carry on the 2021 is positive, which would mean the short future position is likely to have to wait for late delivery. 

We estimate that the switch option is currently worth about 12 cents assuming early delivery, or about 20c assuming late delivery. On balance, we believe the value of the switch option to late delivery outweighs any negative carry, and so the Dec contract looks about 11 cents rich.
