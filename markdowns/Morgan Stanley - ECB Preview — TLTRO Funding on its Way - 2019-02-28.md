## **ECB Preview | Europe** 

## TLTRO Funding on its Way 

The central bank will likely focus on replacing the long-term refinancing operations. Downgrades to the macro path should provide the backdrop for an announcement of extra bank funding, either at the upcoming March meeting or in April. 

**Assessing the policy transmission mechanism:** The Governing Council will probably take a closer look at whether the banking system is still able to transmit any monetary impulse adequately, given macroeconomic and funding pressure. On the whole, we believe that it'll conclude that there's a good case for the ECB to address existing or likely financial fragmentation risks. Mitigating these risks is key to preserve the pass-through of past and future monetary policy easing to the real economy via low or even lower lending rates.

**Cutting growth and inflation forecasts:** While discussions could well take a while longer, the main reason why we expect that TLTRO replacement is forthcoming is that the staff projections will likely show much weaker growth and inflation. This should provide the Governing Council enough context to act. The important thing is that the projections will show subtrend growth and a lesser pick-up in core inflation. 

**Credit easing looking likely:** The case to announce TLTRO replacement at the March meeting is that it'd come together with a rather substantial outlook downgrade – these two go hand in hand. But, as often tends to happen when decisions are complex, it may well be that it takes some extra time. If not March, then we'd think that the discussion would have advanced sufficiently to make an April announcement pretty likely. 

**Details to be decisive for market reaction:** We do not see the same monetary policy reason to 'pay' the banks for almost another half a decade – provided that they lend – as with the previous rounds of long-term bank funding, when financial fragmentation was pervasive. Our base case is that we'll have something like a two-year facility indexed to the refi rate, so that the banks would eventually be charged as and when the ECB hikes. More generous terms will probably be a positive surprise for markets. 

**Rates strategy implications:** Overall, we think that a combination of downward forecast revisions and the possibility of hinting at a possible future change in guidance could create an environment that is supportive of flattener trades, and suggest maintaining a position in Euroswaps 2s10s flatteners to capture moves around the meeting. We think that EGB spreads have room to compress if the market is surprised by the generosity of announcements of extra funds for the banks, but potentially greater room to widen should the pace of discussions around TLTRO replacement disappoint.

## A likely forecast cut 

_Daniele Antonucci, Joao Almeida_ 

**A key event:** The upcoming ECB meeting and press conference on March 7 is likely to be important for two reasons. First, the central bank staff is likely to make significant downward revisions to the growth and inflation projections. Second, the debate on TLTRO replacement is likely to gain momentum, with an announcement of fresh bank loans either next week or on April 10. 

**Forecasting a loss of momentum:** Weaker economic projections will likely prompt the Governing Council to agree on replacing the TLTROs: 

**1. Growth | Slowing below trend:** The technical assumptions are all going to be more favourable relative to the previous forecast round, with lower market rates and, much more so, oil prices – along with a slightly weaker euro trade-weighted exchange rate. However, we think that the new projections will also incorporate slower global trade and growth, and a weaker near-term economic path domestically too. This is likely to imply subtrend growth for a while. 

**2. Inflation | Not much of a rise in core:** Even though the narrative on inflation is unlikely to change significantly, the forecast for both headline and – importantly – core inflation is likely to be revised down. The projections will probably assume continued tightening in the labour market, but less than previously. Wage growth is likely to continue to show an upward trend, although we doubt that the projections can realistically assume an even faster pace than currently. 

**The unquantifiable 'uncertainty':** Beyond the numerical changes, we believe that ECB President Mario Draghi will likely emphasise that there are still many uncertainties out there. It's not that things are necessarily worse, e.g., the US has agreed to delay any tariff increase on China's exports. However, things remain rather fluid when it comes to Brexit and, domestically, the situation is uncertain too.

**A macro reason for extra bank funding:** Executive Board member Peter Praet said in a recent interview that the TLTROs have been useful in dealing with impairments in the transmission of monetary policy. **Not if, but when:** We think it's just a matter of time before TLTRO replacement is announced. And not really a lot of time. March would be a good month to announce a new round of bank funding, in our view, given that it would go together with the downward growth and inflation revisions we expect. That's why we think that chances are quite good. But, given the complexity of the decision and that policy-makers don't seem in a rush, it's possible that the next meeting is only about debating, with an announcement only coming in April.

## **Scenario 1 | No meaningful forecast downgrades, no meaningful shift in guidance** 

Although unlikely, this scenario perhaps has the potential to have the greatest implications for yields in the euro area. In this scenario, the ECB does not revise its growth projections downwards, as markets largely expect, and no shift in forward guidance is obvious: the phrase remains _“through the summer”_ , and the hiking path implied in the guidance more or less stays unchanged. In such a scenario, we'd see potential for a sell-off in the short end as the degree of rate expectations priced into the curve moves higher. 

## **Scenario 2 | Meaningful forecast downgrades, no meaningful shift in guidance** 

In this scenario, the ECB downgrades its economic projections as we and consensus largely expect, yet does not increase the dovishness of its forward guidance concomitantly. In this scenario, the policy path that the central bank is guiding appears more at odds with the macroeconomic outlook, per the ECB's own projections, than it did previously – and perceptions grow on the part of market participants that the ECB has not yet fully appreciated the extent or persistence of the softness. 

**2018 lite:** Such an outcome is analogous to a moderate version of the dynamic in late 2018, when the Fed largely maintained its forward guidance against a backdrop of slowing US data, thus creating the perception that a 'policy mistake' was an increasing possibility. Alongside this dynamic, the Treasury curve flattened significantly. In this scenario the ECB's apparent commitment to its guidance may cause a modest sell-off in the short end, led by a marginal rise in the rate expectations component of yields. However, given the central bank's weaker long-term outlook, we could see the opposite move further out the curve in the 2-5y sector and possibly beyond, producing a degree of flattening overall. However, given that we think downward forecast revisions are largely anticipated by the market, the extent to which downward revisions can generate dovish surprises is limited. 

**Diverging from markets:** In the face of major forecast revisions to the downside, we would expect EGB traders to begin to question how relatively stable forward guidance could be maintained. Of course, there are still legitimate avenues by which the Governing Council could justify an unrevised forward guidance in these circumstances. For one, an attempt to redefine depo hikes as a technical adjustment to the benefit of the banking sector could be employed. Second, as has already been stated in the minutes of the most recent Governing Council meeting, there is still some uncertainty among members about the length and severity of the downturn. 

## **Scenario 3 | No meaningful forecast downgrades, dovish shift in guidance** 

In this scenario, there is no meaningful adjustment to forecasts, but there is a dovish shift in rhetoric from the Governing Council which guides markets towards expecting more accommodative conditions for longer. Of course, precisely how this dovish shift materialises will define the parameters of the market response. Our economists now envision that a change from _“through the summer of 2019”_ to _“through the spring of 2020”_ will occur at some point over the next few meetings as the ECB pushes back its depo rate hiking path. A modest dovish surprise would probably provoke less of a reaction from rates markets than would an equivalent hawkish surprise. Given that the macroeconomic weakness in the euro area is widely understood and a very accommodative policy path is already priced in, we think there is a limit to how much the short end and the belly of sovereign curves could rally in such a scenario.

## **Scenario 4 | Meaningful forecast downgrades, dovish shift in guidance** 

This scenario presents the most dovish surprise – the ECB affirming more or less explicitly that the path of normalisation is going to be much slower and shallower than was forecast one year or even six months ago. Similar to the more moderate scenario above, we think that the extent to which markets can truly be surprised by a dovish shift in rhetoric, given the very slow pace of normalisation that's priced in, is limited. In this scenario, however, the dovish shift in guidance coupled with the revisions to the ECB's longer-term forecasts serve to emphasise the longevity of the dovish outlook. We would expect a more meaningful degree of flattening in the short end and the belly as, potentially, the hikes priced in by the market for mid-to-late 2020 get pushed out even further. In such a scenario, a 2s10s Euroswaps flattener would also be an attractive positioning. 

**TLTRO talk:** Our economists expect a robust discussion around TLTRO replacement at the upcoming meeting, in advance of the announcement of a new LTRO of up to two years linked to the refi rate being announced in the spring. While we think that a renewal of LTRO financing is the most likely outcome, policy-makers have been keen to emphasise in recent communications that a new LTRO, let alone its parameters, is not a done deal. 

**A positive surprise is possible…** We think that the extent to which the market expects some kind of LTRO operation skews the risks around the discussion. In our view, EGBs would be more likely to sell off more aggressively if there were no announcement than they would be to rally were there an announcement. Previous rounds were anticipated to various extents but surprised on the favourability of the terms of the (T)LTRO offer. There is some potential for a repeat to occur, whereby a new LTRO is offered on more favourable terms than anticipated and this causes periphery spreads to tighten markedly. 

**…but there are downside risks:** We think that, lacking any robust discussion of LTROs in the immediate future, there is potential for a substantive widening of core-periphery spreads, as the market reprices the probability of an announcement lower. As noted by Austria's central bank governor Ewald Nowotny, according to Bloomberg, the ECB _“has until the summer”_ to arrange for some sort of provision should it wish to do so. In practice, however, consistent with the ECB's principles of forward guidance, an announcement as late as the end of 2Q requires a degree of discussion ahead of time. The window in which the ECB can engage in this discussion is closing.
