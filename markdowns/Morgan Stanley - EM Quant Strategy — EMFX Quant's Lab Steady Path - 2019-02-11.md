## EMFX Quant's Lab: Steady Path 

VIRP HB remained at +4 despite the small correction in EM. Our RSM stalled after declining for over 4 consecutive weeks, but firmly in the "risk neutral" regime. The models suggest that, while a pause might be underway, EM still has room to rally as the Fed's put keeps volatility in check. 

## **For a full explanation of our models,** see How to read the signals. 

**VIRP GBI EM LC signal:** The model's signal remained at +4, pointing to continued medium-term momentum in EM. While a pause may be underway, the Fed's dovish message will likely keep volatility subdued, something that historically has helped EM as a carry asset class. Any dip back below the 0/+1 would change such assessment. 

Although median VRP risk premia increased for the first time since late December, it remains at very low levels compared to history (within the 15% percentile). 

**EMFX VIRP signals:** The model remains long BRL, TRY, ZAR, and RUB in the highbeta space, yielding a +4 signal. The overall positioning of the model is long BRL, TRY, RUB, and ZAR versus MYR, INR, IDR, and THB (Exhibit 2). 

THB and IDR have a double underweight in the short basket as our two-factor model suggests overbought levels in both currency pairs. In addition, with CNY close to a sell signal, Asia has started to look expensive relative to other regions. 

**Regime-switching model:** The RSM indicator stalled after over 4 weeks of falling, but firmly in the "risk neutral" regime. At current levels, the index would have to decrease ~20pp further before it begins to point to "extreme" optimism.

## VIRP high-beta GBI EM LC signal 

Our index (Exhibit 4) consists of the net number of high-beta (MXN, BRL, TRY, ZAR, and RUB) long/short signals at the end of each week extracted from our VIRP model. The ranking/selection of the high-beta currencies set is achieved by using the beta of the USD factor in EM Quant Strategy: Assessing Risk Premia in EMFX (Part 1): A Two-Factor Model Approach (June 5, 2018). We take only positive or negative signals, i.e., we do not take into account the intensity of the signal to avoid noise stemming from idiosyncratic behaviour in a particular high beta. 

While in theory the index could go from -5 to +5, most of the data in our out-of-sample backtest is concentrated between -2 and +3, which are the signals that we take into account. **We take positive numbers as long signals, zero as neutral, and negative numbers as shorts.**

## EMFX VIRP signals 

The volatility and idiosyncratic risk premia strategy (VIRP) adds up the signals from VRP (Volatility Risk Premia) and IRP (Idiosyncratic Risk Premia) strategies in order to produce a single set of signals to trade on a weekly basis (Exhibit 8 and Exhibit 9). The signals are aimed to be complementary and to mitigate the drawbacks of each of the individual strategies (see EM Quant Strategy: Trading Risk Premia in EMFX (Part 2): A mixed strategy using Volatility Risk Premia (VIRP), November 5, 2018, for more information). **Each signal goes from -2 to +2.** 

**The percentage invested in each signal is proportional to its intensity.** For instance, if the model is long BRL and TRY each with a +1 signal but short CNY with a -2 signal, the model would be long 25% BRL, 25% TRY against short 50% CNY. 

If the model is long BRL and TRY each with a +1 signal but short CNY only with a -1, then the portfolio would be long 33% BRL and 33% TRY but short 33% CNY. As the VRP model selects 40% (out of 17) of the currencies to trade and IRP on average has one signal per week, VIRP on average has nine active signals per week, which would translate into an **average weight for each signal of 11% (1/9) in the weekly portfolio.**

## Regime-switching model (RSM) 

Our RSM Index is an observable time series which consists of six variables that will help us determine the regime shifts (the regimes are unobservable). In this exercise, we built an RSM Index (Ticker MSCEEMRI Index) comprised of different variables that we argue should be representative of changes in risk appetite (Exhibit 10). 

- **VIX:** S&P 500 implied volatility as the most followed vol indicator. It is assumed to capture swings in risk appetite accurately. 

**CVIX:** Basket of FX-implied volatility in developed markets. Should increase as economic cycles decouple, which is usually linked with slower global growth. In addition, pricing of higher FX vol tends to disincentivize carry trades as the Sharpe ratio decreases. 

**MOVE:** Higher rates vol could be induced by higher rates or the end of forward guidance, leading to tighter financial conditions and less appetite for risky assets. 

- **US credit:** Substitute for EM credit. As US credit spreads widen, capital flow competition becomes more intense for EM, leading to wider credit spreads, higher costs of funding and slower growth. We use the average spread of HY and IG bonds. 

- **TED spread:** A measure of USD funding constraints. See USD's Relevance for EMFX, June 5, 2018. 

- **US 10y term premium:** A measure for policy mistakes in developed markets. A sudden increase in term premium such as the taper tantrum period could pose serious stress to EM markets. 

The index is normalized with a 3y moving percentile and goes from 0 to 100%. **When our RSM risk index is below 22%, the model suggests a risk-seeking regime; between 22%** 

**and 55%, our model points to a risk-neutral regime; and above 55%, it determines we are in a risk-averse regime.** The contributions to the EM risk index are shown in Exhibit 11.

We highlight that **what really matters is the regime shift rather than the regime itself.** In other words, when the index crosses from either of the "extremes", i.e., risk seeking or risk aversion, back to "neutral", it tends to be a powerful signal to reduce or increase risk in EM. 

**For instance, whenever the 1m MA crosses from "risk seeking" to neutral, it should be interpreted as a warning signal to lighten up risk in EM. In contrast, when the 1m MA index crosses from "risk aversion" to "neutral", it should be an indication to add EM risk.** 

## SIGNALS 

'Sell' signal 

- **Early sell:** Defined as when the index changes from risk-on to neutral, using as an entry date the day when the index is already in the new regime. 

- **Late sell:** Defined as when the index changes from neutral to risk-off, using as an entry date the day when the index is already in the new regime. 

## 'Buy' signal 

- **Early buy:** Defined as when the index changes from risk-off to neutral, using as an entry date the day when the index is already in the new regime. 

- **Late buy:** Defined as when the index changes from neutral to risk-on, using as an entry date the day when the index is already in the new regime.
