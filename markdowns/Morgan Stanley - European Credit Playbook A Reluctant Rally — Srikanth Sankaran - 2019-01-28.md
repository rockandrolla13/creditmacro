## Executive Summary | **The Bottom Line(s)** 

## **Sticking with the bear market playbook for credit** 

- February 2018 marked the tights for spreads this cycle, in our view. As monetary policy tightens and earnings momentum remains anaemic in Europe, credit markets will continue to face headwinds. 

- We recommend using tactical rallies to improve quality and liquidity of cash portfolios. 

## **Staying liquid and nimble in the rally** 

- We are not convinced that the recent rally is sustainable as the key stress points remain intact. We are also more downbeat on the upcoming earnings season versus the consensus. 

## **A muted returns outlook for the full year, with more two-way volatility** 

- We expected another year of negative returns in 2019, but marking to market our return expectations are modestly better. 

## **Price tiering should remain an important theme as markets punish the weak links** 

- We prefer As over BBBs, BBs over Bs and the 3-5-year part of the curve over longer maturities. We are overweight defensive staples and industrials (selectively), neutral on financials (vs. non-financials) and underweight telecoms. 

- At the single-name level, our thematic concerns focus on issuers with large balance sheets, low financial flexibility and high refinancing needs.

## Executive Summary | **What We Heard from Clients** 

_We discussed the 2019 outlook with over 50 investors in December. We thank everyone for taking time to meet us. The table below summarises the feedback from these meetings, with a particular emphasis on the key areas of disagreement._ 

## **Our view Investor feedback** 

- **Cautious on European credit; recommend reducing beta** 

While **sentiment is downbeat** , we didn’t get the sense of extreme bearishness. Our cautious narrative and expectation for negative returns got little pushback, but the abruptness and severity of the moves in November and December fuelled a **divergence in views** . Investors who were positioned light see the moves as an opportunity to add risk selectively, while others view it as a prelude to more disorderly price action. IG-focused accounts **maintain a preference for financials versus non-fins** , but with lower conviction. In HY, consensus is for **single Bs over BBs** , but large price moves and liquidity concerns have hurt confidence. 

- **Margin pressures to increase fundamental concerns** 

Our expectations around rising inflation and **margin pressures appear to be out of consensus** with credit investors. Many clients argued that double-digit unemployment in Spain and Italy, soft PMI data and the auto sector’s difficulties all point to revenue growth being a bigger challenge. Investors appear more optimistic than us on corporates' pricing power. At the same time, a still ‘OK’ economy and relatively benign leverage trends keep investors more comfortable with corporate fundamentals. This backdrop, coupled with more significant wage pressures in the US, means that **our preference for Europe over the US received little pushback, unlike in previous years.** 

- **BBBs an important stress point** 

The BBB theme was very much in vogue, but **liquidity in smaller issuers was viewed as a bigger challenge than the mega capital structures** . While clients were **also cautious on the large BBB issuers** and the overlap across USD and EUR markets, the tone was more balanced. There appears to be more comfort around the ability of these large issuers to generate free cash flow and cut debt. Nevertheless, there was a lot of interest in the targeted short baskets – higher refinancing needs, financial flexibility constraints and weak fundamentals **. We also received a number of questions on the real estate sector** in particular, where the proliferation of small first-time issuers has been pronounced.

## Executive Summary | **What We Heard from Clients** 

## **Our view** 

## **Investor feedback** 

- **Liquidity conditions to stay challenging** 

This was one area of the outlook that evoked the most vocal agreement. Away from politics, **secondary market liquidity is viewed as the single biggest challenge for credit** . We highlighted that a fifth of the EUR HY nonfin index fell by 10 points or more in 2018, with no clear catalyst in many cases. However, there were mixed views on what that means going forward – some clients were becoming more constructive on single Bs for precisely this reason, while others argued that the next bid could be from distressed buyers, suggesting further meaningful downside. Still others are in wait and see mode – not wanting to sell at current levels but with little conviction to buy more. 

- **Expect limited replacement for TLTRO** 

   - We are also out of consensus on a less generous TLTRO announcement. The key pushback we received is that the ECB cannot ignore the already tight market conditions for smaller Italian banks and it will not wait for the situation to deteriorate further. Our **bank credit analysts’ senior bank bond supply forecast appears to be above what market participants expect** . On the non-financials side, some accounts argued for a significant drop in supply offsetting the lack of ECB purchases. 

- **Outflows set to continue** 

   - Clients share our view that **outflows are likely to continue** . Anecdotally, the realised outflows have not been as bad in HY compared to IG. **Many investors maintain above-average cash balances** (ranging from 7-10% in IG and 5-7% in HY funds). Questions were focused on who the incremental buyer of credit might be and if distressed funds were stepping in yet. Our meetings revealed that **LDI appetite is still measured** , but **BB exposure has come down** and corporate bond **yields are beginning to look interesting** . While our Bund yield forecast is viewed as overly optimistic, clients agreed that it would help credit if it materialised. 

- **Up in quality within HY** 

   - Despite having a diverse range of discussions, we **did not get the sense that investors are embracing our preference for BBs over single Bs yet** . Instead, clients suggested that the BBB downgrade story could be worse for BBs than Bs. In addition, some investors argued that **single B valuations look better now given the 15+ point drops in price, versus BBs that have held up relatively better** in recent months. The preference appears to be for cutting the higher cash price paper. On the financials side, some investors mentioned **reengaging in short-call AT1s** . 

- **Leveraged loans outlook more challenging** 

- There was broad-based agreement on this theme, with **some investors already shifting from loans to bonds** . We received questions on why we still prefer the former. A number of clients believed that earnings misses could be a theme for 2018 vintage loans, given weak issuer quality. Gappy price action post earnings misses could also become more common in loans this year.

## Investment Grade | **Single-Name Screens** 

- Below we list a selection of methods to identify more vulnerable credits within BBBs. For a full list of screens please see our SET 2019 European Credit Outlook. 

- **1) Large BBB issuers with high debt growth:** While European issuers have been more disciplined with balance sheet usage than their US counterparts in this cycle, there are exceptions. 

- **2) BBBs with poor financial flexibility:** For issuers, the willingness to maintain an IG rating may be a strategic choice, but one way to measure the ability to do so is the financial flexibility of the corporate. We define this measure as the total of LTM free cash flow, dividends and surplus capital expenditure all relative to total debt. 

- **3) BBBs with front-ended maturities:** We also remain focused on rollover risks and believe that selling bonds of BBB credits with near-term refinancing needs – either outright or as a switch into their more recent or upcoming new issues – makes sense. 

- **4) Weak balance sheet + exposure to negative earnings surprises:** Issuers have been given the benefit of the doubt by investors and ratings agencies with regards to elevated leverage, particularly if it was acquisition-related. However, in recent months we have seen increased pressure on issuers with more stretched balance sheets when earnings disappoint. We believe that this theme will persist in 2019.

## Financials | **Staying Up in Issuer Quality but Prefer Discounted Sub Debt** 

_For the 2019 European Banks Credit Outlook, see A Port in a Storm from Jackie Ineke_ 

**1H will be tricky given a potential deluge of senior supply, the first AT1 non-call, the conclusion of CRR2 and half a trillion of TLTRO casting its shadow over markets. Despite all this, we remain equal-weight overall, but move underweight seniors and, perhaps counterintuitively, overweight AT1s** 

- **Underweight seniors:** Supply is likely to be a constant vulnerability this year, at least for 1Q19. While our economists believe there will be a fine-tuning operation to succeed the TLTRO, it’s likely to be announced in response to growing funding strains, in our view, meaning spreads are going wider. 

- **Equal-weight Tier 2:** Our view of any resolution requiring a buyer who would demand bail-in of every single bail-inable instrument possible means that non-preferreds should trade far closer to Tier 2. They don’t, and haven’t for some time now, but with a combination of English law making first calls in Tier 2s more certain, cheaper valuations and net negative issuance, we’re equal-weight on the cleanest, most boring credits – with added English law upside. 

- **Overweight ‘no hope of a first call’ AT1s** , adding some liquidity and carry to our recommendations. In a market which may simply grind wider between bursts of volatility, we are happy taking carry on discount AT1s of the most solid issuers. Add in regulatory par call fears linked to CRR2, and we have no AT1 buys above par, and plenty of sells. 

- **Not changing our Italian and UK bank recommendations** , nor are we buying back into anything with material Italian or Turkish exposure. Having been underweight the largest bank credit sector (the UK) for nearly a year now, we still don’t believe that it’s time to add just yet. We sold out of anything remotely peripheryrelated in May but, with the observable de-linkage of quality second tier Spanish from Italian bank spread movements, we add their Tier 2s back now.

## Where We Could Be Wrong | **Bull and Bear Scenarios** 

- The themes and recommendations above outline our thinking around the base case (60% subjective probability). But as always, there are risks around this narrative – bullish and bearish. 

- **The bull case – a longer cycle:** Consecutive years of negative excess returns are rare outside a recession. In our bull case, credit tests the weak links but they hold for longer against the backdrop of a stronger-than-expected rebound in global growth. Key signposts to watch in this context would be expansionary fiscal policy in Europe that could improve the long-term outlook and/or earnings trends holding up better than our expectations. An alternate path to our bull case would be for the weak growth patch to force a more dovish response from central banks. The Fed slows down the pace of tightening, the ECB renews TLTRO and the cycle continues for longer. 

- **The bear case – politics and trade:** The bear case of our economists’ narrative is a mild recession in late 2019. And more importantly, key drivers of this risk scenario are US corporate sector risks and a deterioration in trade relations. As always, political risks in Europe (Italy, UK and Germany in particular) are another path to our bear case. If trade dominates, the pain will be routed through the corporate sector, while the rise in political premiums would be more significant in the financial sector. In both cases, the alignment of technicals and fundamentals is likely to deteriorate significantly for European credit. We model spreads to test the bottom end of the sovereign crisis in this scenario.
