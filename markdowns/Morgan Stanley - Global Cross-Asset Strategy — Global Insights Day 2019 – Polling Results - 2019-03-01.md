## Summary 

Our key observations from the informal polling at our Global Insights conference in New York this morning (March 1, 2019) are as follows: 

**Moderate caution:** 23% of NY investors described their current level of risk exposure as 'lower than normal' versus 18% who describe it as 'higher than normal'. In comparison, at our London event in January, European investors responded with 38% and 15%, respectively, which is the most cautious response to this question recorded since we started asking it in 2012 across the pond. 

**Bullish on the S&P:** 78% of investors anticipate the S&P rounding out 2019 at 2800 or above, representing more bullish sentiment than our strategists hold, who see fair value as range-bound from 2500 to 2800. 

**No love for the UK or Japan:** No investors chose the UK as their preferred equity region for the next 12 months, compared to 22% who chose the UK at our London event. Japan is also an unfavoured region, with just 3% of NY investors denoting it as their preferred region. On the flip side, our strategists see promise in European, Japanese and Chinese equities; part of the reason behind our strategists being bullish on European equity is in fact _because_ it is so unloved and overlooked (as our polling results show). 

**Preference for technology:** Our investors showed a bias towards technology, with 35% choosing it as their preferred global equity sector. In contrast, their European counterparts expressed no clear bias at the sector level. 

**Expectation for higher Treasury yields:** Overall, 67% of investors said that Treasury yields are likely to end 2019 higher than current levels. 53% of investors expect the 10y UST to end 2019 at 2.80% or higher. In contrast, our rate strategists' base case is for the 10y UST to end the year at 2.45%. Only 16% of investors expressed the belief that 10y UST would end up in that range (2.20-2.50%). However, to caveat that, most investors (68%) do feel that we've already seen the highs in 10y UST yields for this cycle at 3.25%. 

**Recession and yield curve inversion:** Investors are split on the timing of the next US recession (with 35% saying 2021 or later vs. 44% saying in the second half of 2020). 75% of investors say that they expect the yield curve (2s10s) to invert before the next recession. If the yield curve were to invert, the vast majority of investors (95%) see the start of the next recession within two years of the inversion. 

**Wider US credit:** Our investors agree with our US credit strategists and see US IG credit spreads going wider through the end of 2019. 70% of clients anticipate spreads to widen vs. 30% anticipating them to tighten. Leveraged loans (40%) followed by IG BBB nonfinancials (29%) were identified as sectors where US credit quality has deteriorated the most in this cycle. 

**Neutral to underweight duration:** 35% of polled investors are currently positioned underweight duration vs. 17% positioned overweight. 

2 

**Economic nationalism and US/China world as greatest disruptions:** Investors saw economic nationalism and a shift from a unipolar (US) to bipolar (US/China) world as the biggest potential disruption risks to the global economy. 

**US fiscal, healthcare and auto tariff policy:** Zero investors foresee a substantial reduction to the US deficit after the next election. Most expect US healthcare policy to include a public option with increased drug price negotiation/control going forward. 80% of investors anticipate US auto tariffs being either a removed risk or non-implemented threat, with few seeing it actually being applied in 2019. 

**Factor investing surprise:** In the context of factor investing, investors were most surprised by the broad underperformance of all factors. Three-quarters of investors did not see factor investing as the primary driver of the volatile swings in the markets in 2018. 

**Promising quant lands:** Investors foresee a swathe of promising avenues for quant investing in 2019, with expansion into new asset classes, use of alternative data and application of advanced methodologies being the top three avenues.

Mortgage Backed Securities (MBS) and Collateralized Mortgage Obligations (CMO) 

Principal is returned on a monthly basis over the life of the security. Principal prepayment can significantly affect the monthly income stream and the maturity of any type of MBS, including standard MBS, CMOs and Lottery Bonds. Yields and average lives are estimated based on prepayment assumptions and are subject to change based on actual prepayment of the mortgages in the underlying pools. The level of predictability of an MBS/CMO's average life, and its market price, depends on the type of MBS/CMO class purchased and interest rate movements. In general, as interest rates fall, prepayment speeds are likely to increase, thus shortening the MBS/CMO's average life and likely causing its market price to rise. Conversely, as interest rates rise, prepayment speeds are likely to decrease, thus lengthening average life and likely causing the MBS/CMO's market price to fall. Some MBS/CMOs may have "original issue discount" (OID). OID occurs if the MBS/CMO’s original issue price is below its stated redemption price at maturity, and results in "imputed interest" that must be reported annually for tax purposes, resulting in a tax liability even though interest was not received. Investors are urged to consult their tax advisors for more information. Government agency backing applies only to the face value of the CMO and not to any premium paid.
