## **What’s Notable This Week:** 

- The co-ordinated sell-off in developed market government bonds that began at the end of June continued for a second week last week. Our rates strategists believe that the move in Bunds was driven by positioning shifts rather than a substantial reassessment of the monetary policy outlook. On the data front, Wednesday’s euro area industrial production should indicate a marked acceleration and set the stage for one of the best quarters since 2011. Meanwhile, the UK unemployment rate is expected to fall 0.1pp to 4.5%. 

- The traditional negative correlation between credit spreads and bond yields has reasserted itself in recent weeks. EUR IG spreads tightened by 4bp over the last week, with financials modestly outperforming non-financials. HY was less resilient amid the weaker summer liquidity and index spreads closed the week 9bp wider. Separately, we recently took stock of cross-currency relative value, particularly given the large volume of reverse Yankee supply in 2Q17 (see Corporate Bond Cross-Currency Tracker). 

- European IG mutual funds experienced flat net flows, while HY funds saw outflows equivalent to 1.7% of AUM last week.
