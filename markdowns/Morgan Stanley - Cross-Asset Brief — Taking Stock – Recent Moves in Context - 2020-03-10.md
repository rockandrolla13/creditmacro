## Taking stock of recent moves 

Following two weeks of large moves across asset classes and regions, yesterday saw even larger one-day moves across major asset classes, with the moves in a number of key assets comparable to those last seen in 2008. We update our snapshots of where assets stand, what stands out across equities, credit, rates, FX, commodities and volatility, and where these big moves leave risk premiums. 

## Which assets underperformed the most? 

Brent saw its largest decline since the Gulf War in 1991. S&P energy stocks followed, with the index seeing its worst performance in history. On a relative basis, staples, telcos, healthcare and utilities fared better across regions. While stocks globally took large hits, ex-US regions did marginally better. US stocks saw their worst performance since 2008, while Topix and Eurostoxx 50 saw their worst performance since 2016. US IG credit posted its worst daily return since the financial crisis, and CDX HY experienced its worst day since the inception of the index in 2007. Long-end Treasuries saw their best daily return since the financial crisis, gilt yields turned negative and gold reached a five-year high.

## Where are risk premiums now? 

Declines in equity markets over the past two weeks were accompanied by a sharp fall in government bond yields. While valuations may look average, _relative valuations_ (i.e., risk premiums) are now extreme in certain markets. We take stock of how current levels of risk premium in both equity and credit look across the major regions. 

## Equity risk premiums now look elevated 

There are many acceptable ways to define equity risk premium. In the interest of being able to cover a broad set of markets, we use a simple method below – the difference between cyclically adjusted earnings yield and nominal 10y yield of the respective country. 

Given the moves over the last two weeks, equity risk premiums now look extremely high across most regions. Notably, Japan, the UK, Australia and Singapore now have extremely high equity risk premiums, while equity risk premium in Italy is still below average.

## Volatility: Fixed income volatility catches up 

Nearly all asset class vols are in the top decile of their 15-year history and pricing implied daily moves that set a very high bar for vol buyers and are likely hard to realise over three months. Implied vols for the S&P 500 are now priced for a 2.5% implied daily move. But the shifts in fixed income vol were dramatic as CDX IG is now priced for a 7.0bp implied daily move now versus ~1.1bp at the start of the year. Brent now prices a 4.8% implied daily move. US 10yr rates are now pricing a 8.6bp implied daily move. The big divergences between equity/credit vol and rates/FX volatility are reducing rapidly. 

Skew and term structures are close to levels seen at the peak of the credit crisis due to the sharp moves. HY credit 25delta skew is now steeper in absolute terms than for the S&P 500 as credit posted the single worst one-day drop since 2008.
