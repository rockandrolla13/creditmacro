## Introducing the Alaph Capital Liquid Macro Credit Fund 

- The Alaph Capital Liquid Macro Credit Fund [“The Fund”] is an actively traded vehicle which takes a top-down oriented approach. The Fund expresses medium-term investment views within the global credit markets with a distinct overlay of macro products. 

- This unique approach identifies actionable themes that drive market behaviour and creates a systematic ranking of relative value opportunities in terms of their risk reward, to select trades and allocate capital in a consistent and optimal fashion. 

- Fundamental credit analysis validates specific name exposures, while portfolio construction seeks to maximize diversification across themes and strategies. 

- The process is scalable and repeatable, generating excess returns across all stages of the business cycle. 

- The Fund has a strong risk culture with an absolute focus on liquidity and its ability to convert positions into cash. 

- There are two expressions of trading style; a medium term discretionary approach with a typical holding period of one week to three months, and a shorter term tactical approach to capture opportunities over shorter time frames. 

- The Fund targets a risk return of 1:2 or better.

## Criteria for Trade Construction and Sizing 

- Focus on structuring trades with pre-defined risks  (more important than the strength of conviction) 

- Expected time to thesis validation (period of time before trade thesis is either proven right or wrong) 

- Evaluation of base-case P&L relative to expected worse case scenario 

- Consideration of liquidity costs in case of forced exit

- VaR and other risk limits 

- Relation of the trade to the remainder of the portfolio

- Every position in the portfolio is consistently re-balanced/re-evaluated with a focus on value extraction 

- Robust proprietary technology provides comprehensive and exhaustive daily monitoring and review

## **How to think about Returns and Risk**

   - Break down returns in two components:  Market Risk and Structural Risk

   - Liquidity risk is one of our fundamental metrics – measure it by assigning a number on it that ties it to cost and time of exit, with pre-set limits on both.

## Proprietary Liquidity Scoring Mechanism 

- ‘Liquidity’ is the ability to exit a position and convert it to cash easily and with the least cost. Liquidity is characterized by a high level of trading activity. 

- Trade only the instruments that allow you to be liquid as measured in cost and time 

- What characteristics does a portfolio of eligible instruments have: 

   - Ability to exit a minimum of 80% of the portfolio within 15-20 trading days irrespective of market conditions 

   - Limit on the absolute P&L cost to exit half the position at market levels 

   - Limit on the time to exit half the position at a predefined P&L impact (e.g. 1.5x the normal bid offer cost) 

- Proprietary ranking methodology ranks the potential universe based on a scoring mechanism which takes observable market inputs: frequency of activity in the name/instrument, trade volume, number of clearing participants, costs and the volatility of market moves 

- **Inputs** 

- **Single Name Activity** 

- Frequency of dealer quotes  Number of participating dealers  Average notional traded per week  Average number of trades per week 

- **Transaction Costs Uncertainty** 

- Bid/offer spread  Volatility measure  Bid/offer spread dispersion

## Long / Short – Long risk France vs. Short risk French banks 

## Description 

- Short risk (long CDS) on French banks vs. long risk (short CDS) on France 

   - Short risk on a equal-weighted basket of French Banks through owning senior CDS on BNP Paribas, Societe Generale and Credit Agricole 

   - Long risk on the beta-neutral equivalent amount of France CDS 

## Step 1: Development of Macro Themes 

- Macroeconomic Fundamentals: Prior transfers of bank liabilities to government balance sheets through funding guarantees and capital injections  overextended European sovereigns with weak fiscal health 

- Business cycle analysis: Weak economic growth leading to (a) weak credit demand and (b) worsening asset quality for banks 

- Monetary and fiscal policy : Regulation (e.g., Basel 3) requiring banks to delever, extend funding maturities and raise capital 

- Drivers of asset class performance: Meaningful exposure of banking system to peripheral sovereign debt and rating downgrade pressures 

- Specific market theme: Decreasing sovereign support for European banking system in the face of deteriorating sector fundamentals 

## Step 2: Asset Valuation 

- Market pricing: French bank risk premium, as measured by difference in senior CDS spread of French banks vs. French sovereign, identified as inadequate 

- Valuation analysis: 

- Spread difference (at c. 40bps) near its own historical low 

- Premium low relative to the broader European financials market (see  graph to the right) 

- Premium low relative to other core countries[(b)] – with German banks at around 86bps and UK banks at 112bps spread difference vs. their respective sovereigns
