## **If an Issuer Gets Junked, Could the Hybrid Be Called at 101?** 

- _Potentially_ , **but there’s some doubt whether RECs would even be triggered for the majority of the European hybrids universe issued since 2010,** because of the way the RECs are worded. 

- And even _if_ a REC is triggered, there are additional factors, generally economic, which issuers need to consider: 

   - **Call price:** If the early redemption price is significantly above market levels, it may not be in a company’s economic interest to call the hybrid, even if it no longer carries equity benefit. 

   - **If the issuer has been junked, the cost of refinancing a hybrid with senior paper can be great:** Exercising a 101 call requires the downgraded issuer to put up cash, or issue other debt. The former may be unavailable around a downgrade, while the latter may be expensive.

From an issuer’s perspective, the fact that a hybrid security still provides _some_ relief from at least one agency may be reason enough not to redeem early. 

- Note that these reasons for an issuer not to exercise the option to redeem early on a Rating Event are extremely similar to those that an issuer faces at the first call. If a company’s credit profile is significantly weak around the first call date, factors such as refinancing costs and whether equity credit is intact at other rating agencies could sway companies into a non-call.

## **Coupon step-ups** 

**What?:** If not called at this first call date, the coupon typically resets to a Libor or swapslinked coupon, usually L + Margin where the Margin equals the spread at issuance. If the hybrid is not called at the second call date, the coupon resets again, usually to L + Margin + 25-100bp. (p17)

**What?:** Replacement language generally requires issuers to redeem the existing hybrid only with funds from newly raised hybrid or common stock. (p19)

## **What Are Some of the Ke Features of Cor orate H brids? y p y** 

**Structural Subordination** 

Almost all corporate hybrids **rank junior to unsecured debt** in the event of insolvency, and with no limitation on the issuance of more senior debt above the hybrid. As such, we assume that the recovery on a hybrid in the event of a default is similar to equity (and close to zero). 

**Coupon Deferral and Other Arrangements** 

In almost all cases, **coupons can be deferred** _**at the discretion**_ **of the company** . These skipped coupons **may accrue** _**cumulatively**_ in the case of optional deferral, and generally must be paid in full – and sometimes with interest – as a prerequisite for paying a dividend to common or preferred stock. 

Interest can be deferred for a substantial period (often five years) before constituting a default under the securities, although at this time they would become immediately payable by the issuer. 

**Structural subordination and coupon deferral also mean that corporate hybrids are generally rated two notches below senior unsecured debt from the same IG issuer.**

**Rating agencies tend to treat a portion of hybrids as debt and a portion as equity (the ‘equity credit’),** _**as long as the hybrid security possesses equity-like features**_ **.** How this is assigned, and how much is assigned varies across the agencies. 

Rating agencies have set some limits on the amount of hybrids that would be recognised. For Moody’s, hybrids beyond 25% of equity capitalisation would be treated as debt. For S&P, it’s a limit of 15% of total capitalisation (i.e., equity + hybrids + debt). Fitch has no explicit limit, although it would make adjustments should it view the amount of hybrid capital as excessive. 

**The loss of equity credit from rating agencies can be viewed as an incentive for issuers to redeem** 

**hybrids before this occurs.** Otherwise, hybrids which are left outstanding become an expensive form of debt and add to corporates’ leverage ratios as calculated by the rating agencies. **For most of the newly issued hybrids, the point at which equity credit falls away is the first call date.** A change in rating agencies’ treatment of equity credit also triggers optional early redemption for many European corporate hybrids.
